## Lecture Outline

### Core Concepts: Mootness and Article III

Mootness derives from Article III's "Case/Controversy" requirement -- there is no jurisdiction if the case is not "live," and the doctrine prevents advisory opinions *(p. 2)*. Voluntary cessation operates as the central exception: a case is moot only if (1) there is no reasonable expectation of recurrence and (2) there are no lingering effects of the violation *(p. 2)*. The defendant bears the burden of proving mootness, and the bar is high *(p. 2)*.

### The Case Catalog: Power to Revert

**W.T. Grant Co. (1953):** An antitrust case in which the defendant voluntarily ceased ceased interlocking directorates *(p. 3)*. The Court held the case was not moot -- cessation does not equal mootness, and the test is "reasonable expectation" of repeat wrong *(p. 3)*. The potential for resuming similar director roles meant the controversy was still live *(p. 3)*.

**City of Mesquite v. Aladdin's Castle (1982):** A city amended a challenged ordinance *(p. 3)*. The Court held the case was not moot because the city could "return to old ways" -- the unilateral power to revert matters *(p. 3)*. Unilateral power to resume means no mootness *(p. 3)*.

### Policy Change and Regulatory Rollback

**Trinity Lutheran Church of Columbia v. Comer (2017):** Missouri changed its grant-denial policy after the case was filed *(p. 4)*. The Court held not moot because a policy change is not permanent enough -- a binding guarantee for policy changes is needed *(p. 4)*.

**West Virginia v. EPA (2022):** EPA repealed the Clean Power Plan *(p. 4)*. The Court held not moot because the underlying EPA authority dispute persists -- the broader controversy outlives the specific action *(p. 4)*.

### The Rare Mootness Finding

**Already, LLC v. Nike (2013):** Nike issued a covenant not to sue Already in a patent dispute *(p. 5)*. The Court held the case moot because an irrevocable covenant eliminates the controversy -- the rare exception where a binding covenant equals mootness *(p. 5)*.

**Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw Environmental Services (2000):** Laidlaw complied and ultimately closed the facility *(p. 5)*. The Court held not moot because penalties deter and redress injury, and compliance after suit does not equal mootness *(p. 5)*.

### Strategic Mootness at the Supreme Court

**New York State Rifle and Pistol Association v. City of New York (2020):** Gun transport restrictions were modified by city and state action *(p. 5)*. The Court explicitly found the case moot because the combined city and state actions made the cessation permanent *(p. 5)*.

**Moore v. Harper (2023):** A gerrymandering challenge in which North Carolina's court and legislature both acted *(p. 6)*. The Court held the case moot because state court and legislature actions made cessation permanent -- mootness via state judicial and legislative action *(p. 6)*.

### The Simplified Three-Factor Predictive Framework

The deck synthesizes the case law into a three-factor analytical framework *(p. 8)*:

1. **Binding and Permanent Cessation?** Cessation via statute, state supreme court ruling, or irrevocable covenant tilts toward mootness because such actions are hard to reverse; policy changes, ordinances, or reversible actions tilt away because the defendant retains power *(p. 8)*.

2. **Full Harm Undone?** If conduct has stopped, past harm has been remedied, and no lingering effects remain, mootness is more likely; partial cessation, lingering harm, or relevant deterrence cuts the other way *(p. 8)*.

3. **Strategic Maneuver?** Genuine motivation for cessation slightly favors mootness; cessation timed to manipulate jurisdiction slightly disfavors it *(p. 8)*.

### The Clapper Tension

The lecture closes by surfacing a deep tension in justiciability doctrine *(p. 7)*. Clapper (standing) is strict and hyper-technical, demanding probability of harm and rejecting "speculation"; voluntary cessation (especially NYSRPA mootness) is pragmatic, finding mootness based on the elimination of present harm through cessation *(p. 7)*. The doctrines diverge starkly -- probability of harm in Clapper versus elimination of harm in NYSRPA -- raising the question of "manipulable justiciability" and a cynical view of justiciability as "shadow boxing" on the merits *(p. 7)*. The charitable view: the doctrines address different Article III concerns, but the tension is pronounced *(p. 7)*. This page reads NYSRPA in particular as a politically controversial Second Amendment case mooted before the Court could rule on the merits -- an editor's gloss that goes beyond what the deck states.

## Hypotheticals and Class Discussion

The deck presents a three-factor framework for predicting mootness outcomes *(p. 8)*. The key application exercise: given a fact pattern involving defendant cessation, work through (1) whether the cessation is binding and permanent (legislative? irrevocable covenant? or mere policy?), (2) whether the full harm is undone (lingering effects? pending penalties?), and (3) whether there is any indication of strategic maneuvering to escape adverse ruling while retaining the ability to resume.

Exam tip: If a defendant changes conduct after a case is filed, your first instinct is "not moot." Follow with the three-factor test. The only clean mootness findings involve irrevocable covenants (Already) or legislative action removing the defendant's power to resume (NYSRPA, Moore v. Harper).

## Key Takeaways

- The voluntary cessation exception is the rule in practice: defendant cessation almost never moots a case unless cessation is demonstrably permanent and the defendant cannot unilaterally revive the challenged conduct.
- The defendant bears the burden of proving mootness -- and it is a heavy burden.
- Legislative or state-court action can make cessation genuinely permanent (NYSRPA, Moore v. Harper); a policy change or ordinance amendment almost never does.
- Penalties and deterrence are independent forms of relief that keep cases alive even after full compliance (Laidlaw).
- The Clapper-voluntary-cessation tension is a live doctrinal fault line worth noting in exam analysis.
