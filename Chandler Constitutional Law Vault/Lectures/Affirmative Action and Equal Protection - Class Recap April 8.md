## Lecture Outline

### Three Theories of Affirmative Action

Three rationales have been offered for race-conscious preferences in government programs and higher education (slide 2):

**Specific reparations.** The institution itself discriminated and must undo the harm — still accepted, but rarely triggered because it is hard to find institutions with provable past discrimination today (slide 2).

**General reparations.** Society broadly discriminated (slavery, Jim Crow, redlining), so government should compensate; rejected in Bakke and never accepted by any majority, with tracing and line-drawing problems dooming it (slide 2).

**Utilitarian / diversity.** Racial mixing yields better education, leadership, and social cohesion; accepted by Powell in Bakke, endorsed in Grutter, then dismantled in SFFA — and the evidence turned out to be weak (slide 2). The diversity rationale's primary beneficiaries are majority students who gain exposure — a framework that opponents characterized as using minority admissions to improve the education of white students.

**The core tension.** Anti-subordination (EP Clause lifts up oppressed groups) versus anti-classification (EP Clause forbids ALL racial sorting) — and anti-classification has won (slide 2).

### Bakke (1978): The Foundation

Powell's plurality (in a 4-1-4 split) applied strict scrutiny to all racial classifications — even those helping minorities; EP protects individuals, not groups (slide 3). The UC Davis program reserved 16 of 100 seats for minority applicants and was unconstitutional because Bakke could never compete for those seats (slide 3). General reparations were rejected as "amorphous injury reaching endlessly into the past" (slide 3). But diversity survived as a compelling interest, rooted in academic freedom and the First Amendment (slide 3). The Harvard Model — race as a "plus factor" in holistic review, like playing the trumpet or being a legacy, with all applicants competing for every seat — is constitutional (slide 3). This produced the diversity irony: the rationale's real beneficiaries are white students, with minorities admitted to enrich everyone's education — a framework believers in general reparations called a "repugnant theory" (slide 3). Powell hoped this would be unnecessary within 25 years (slide 3).

### Adarand, Grutter, Gratz, and Schuette

Adarand (1995): a federal highway program gave bonuses for hiring minority subcontractors, and the Court held that strict scrutiny applies to ALL racial classifications — federal, state, local (slide 4). The diversity rationale is weak outside education: there is no "educational benefit" from diverse guardrail contractors, and the deck flags this as where the seeds of the end of affirmative action were planted (slide 4).

Grutter (Law School, upheld): holistic review, race as one factor, no fixed points; O'Connor endorses Powell's diversity rationale as law, with the "We expect 25 years from now…" sunset language (slide 4).

Gratz (Undergrad, struck down): a 150-point index with 20 automatic points for minority status; mechanical, no individualized review, looking like Bakke's quota (slide 4). Ginsburg's dissent captured the transparency paradox — Michigan was just being honest; why punish candor while rewarding "winks, nods, and disguises"? (slide 4).

Schuette (2014, 6-2): Michigan voters banned affirmative action by state constitutional amendment; diversity may justify AA but cannot compel it, so voters may ban preferences (slide 4). Sotomayor's retort to Roberts: "The way to stop discrimination on the basis of race is to speak openly and candidly on the subject of race" (slide 4).

### SFFA v Harvard (2023): The Reckoning

The Court struck down Harvard's admissions program on five grounds that together constitute a comprehensive dismantling of the diversity rationale (slide 5):

(1) **Judicial unmanageability.** Diversity goals ("training leaders," "new knowledge") are not judicially manageable — courts can't measure them; shades of Rucho (slide 5).

(2) **Racial balancing in disguise.** Harvard's enrollment was suspiciously stable year to year, looking like racial balancing, not holistic review (slide 5).

(3) **Zero-sum harm to Asian Americans.** Admissions is zero-sum — advantages for some equal disadvantages for others — and Asian Americans bore the brunt, with an 11% decrease in admissions (slide 5).

(4) **Stereotyping.** Using race equals stereotyping: assuming a Black student brings something a white student can't reduces identity to skin color (slide 5).

(5) **No end point.** Harvard refused to give a sunset date, and O'Connor's 25-year hope became a rule (slide 5).

Harvard's "LOP" process: the final winnowing used only four variables — legacy status, athlete status, financial aid, and race; race entered at every stage (slide 5). Admissions officers gave Asian applicants lower "personality" scores despite stronger objective credentials (slide 5).

Justice Harlan's dissent in Plessy finally becomes the majority: "Our Constitution is color-blind" (slide 5). Harvard (private) is swept in via Title VI — the Court assumes the statutory standard mirrors the constitutional one — and anti-classification wins definitively (slide 5).

### The Post-SFFA Landscape

The essay exception: race info is admissible only to corroborate hardship claims tied to character or unique ability (slide 6). "I'm Latina, admit me" is impermissible; "I'm Latina and my underfunded school limited my prep" is permissible — but Roberts warns against using essays as a backdoor for racial classification (slide 6).

The military academy footnote: the Court reserves the question of race-based admissions at West Point, Annapolis, etc., on a national security rationale that diverse officers should lead diverse troops (slide 6). Currently moot — the Trump administration ended military AA — but the footnote remains a potential wedge (slide 6).

Most law schools now use race-blind admissions (slide 6). Race-neutral alternatives are proliferating: percentage plans, SES preferences, dropping legacy admits (slide 6). Ginsburg's prediction — diversity through "winks, nods, and disguises" — will be tested by SFFA's anti-circumvention principle (slide 6). The post-SFFA doctrinal frontier is whether facially neutral proxies that predictably produce racial diversity trigger Equal Protection or Title VI challenge.

## Hypotheticals and Class Discussion

**The Marshall Legacy Scholarship Problem.** Private law firms fund a race-conscious scholarship at a state law school. Is the school's cooperation enough to constitute state action, triggering Equal Protection scrutiny? The recap identifies two lines of cases pulling in opposite directions: Burton v Wilmington (symbiosis supports state action where the school promotes, provides conference room space, and advertises the scholarship) versus Rendell-Baker v Kohn (state action denied because the school did not select recipients -- no decision-making authority).<!-- corrected 2026-05-26: was "Blum v Yaretsky"; the source deck cites Rendell-Baker v. Kohn --> The bottom line is probably no state action, but a risk-averse dean would cut conference room use and reduce active promotion. Exam tip: the Marshall Legacy problem is a model for a state action / equal protection crossover question.

**The Transparency Paradox.** Ginsburg's Gratz dissent asks why candor should be penalized. Michigan's 20-point index made explicit what Harvard's holistic review concealed. If both programs achieve the same result -- minority enrollment at roughly the same percentage -- why should one be constitutional and the other not? The answer in SFFA is that racial balancing itself (not just explicit quotas) is impermissible, so the stable-enrollment-pattern problem in Grutter/Harvard was built in to any serious diversity program. The transparency paradox thus collapses: both programs fail, but for different stated reasons.

## Key Takeaways

- The anti-classification theory has decisively won: race-conscious government action receives strict scrutiny whether it burdens or benefits minorities.
- Diversity is no longer a compelling interest sufficient to justify individual racial classification in higher education admissions.
- The post-SFFA doctrinal frontier is the essay exception and the anti-circumvention principle: race-neutral proxies that predictably produce racial diversity may themselves be challenged.
- The Grutter 25-year sunset was a structural time bomb; SFFA detonated it 20 years early.
- Exam note: SFFA does not overrule the diversity rationale in all contexts (military academies still reserved); its holding is specific to undergraduate and graduate admissions programs that classify individual applicants by race.
