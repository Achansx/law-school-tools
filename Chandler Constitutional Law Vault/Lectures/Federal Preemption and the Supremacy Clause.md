## Lecture Outline

**Constitutional Foundation: The Supremacy Clause**

Article VI, Clause 2 declares the Constitution, federal statutes, and treaties the supreme Law of the Land and binds state judges to them notwithstanding contrary state law (slide 2). This clause is self-executing in the sense that a conflicting state law yields to federal law without any additional congressional action. But it does not tell courts when a state law is actually inconsistent with federal law -- that is the preemption inquiry.

**Taxonomy of Preemption**

The deck shows a "preemption tree" (slide 3) and notes the linguistic gradient from "relates to" (broadest) through "with respect to" and "covering" (slide 6). Express preemption occurs when a federal statute explicitly displaces state law, and the linguistic precision of the clause drives the analysis. Field preemption applies when Congress has so thoroughly legislated a domain that it implicitly occupied the entire field. Conflict preemption has two subtypes: impossibility and obstacle. Physical conflict preemption is VERY rare in the real world (slide 16); most conflict preemption cases involve the obstacle variant.

**Presumption Against Preemption**

CRS believes the presumption is unclear; the professor's reading is that SCOTUS was only saying that if a federal statute speaks in unmistakable language, state laws are preempted without invoking the presumption (slide 5). The presumption is weaker in subjects the states have not traditionally regulated, in fraud against federal agencies, and in areas where the federal government has traditionally had a significant regulatory presence -- maritime, national banks, lighthouses, military academies, and maybe railroads (slide 5).

Sub-state laws (municipalities, counties) are subject to preemption; territories don't exactly have sovereignty, so the analysis depends on the particular territory's governing laws; Indian tribal law is a separate large topic (slide 9).

**Procedural Implications**

State tort claims can be preempted by federal safety statutes: in the Cara hypo, a passenger is injured by a freight train traveling 20 mph in fog (below the 25 mph permitted under the Federal Rail Safety Act, which takes track curvature and physical factors into account but does not restrict speed for weather) and sues in Texas state court (slide 7). The procedural questions are whether the train operator may remove to federal court and, if not, whether it may assert FRSA preemption as a state-court defense (slide 7). In the ERISA hypo, Wally has employer-provided health insurance funded by a trust with a TPA, sues the trust in Texas state court for breach of contract and bad faith seeking emotional distress and punitive damages (permitted under Texas law) -- ERISA permits beneficiary suits but bars punitive damages, so the question is whether the case may be removed and, if not, whether extra-contractual damages may be dismissed as conflicting with ERISA (slide 8).

**Selected Hypos**

The Ukraine Freedom Support Act hypo: Congress required the President to impose at least three of several sanctions on Russia-owned or Russian-linked businesses, with broad National Security waiver authority used by Trump to waive sanctions against Rusal and two other Deripaska-linked companies after they accepted transparency measures; Pennsylvania's hypothetical "Stop Russian Aggression Act" bars state entities from buying from those on a "restricted purchase list" of businesses doing business with Russia, and Rusal had been selling aluminum to Pennsylvania for guardrails (slide 11). Could Rusal successfully challenge the Pennsylvania law (slide 11)?

The Cebotari refugee hypo: a Moldovan refugee with a valid I-94 (more than two years old, but never converted to LPR status) is denied a Minnesota driver's license under a state "current document" law requiring an I-94 less than two years old (slide 12). Will a challenge to the Minnesota current-document law succeed (slide 12)?

The Thanks, Tesla Act of 2022 hypo: Texas imposes a 10% surcharge on sales tax for any electric car purchased in Texas not substantially manufactured in Texas; Rivian (Illinois/Georgia manufacturing) finds the tax conflicts with U.S. free-trade policy (slide 13). What is Rivian's best basis for challenge (slide 13)?

The Cigarette Labels hypo: 15 U.S.C. 1334 of the FCLAA preempts any "requirement or prohibition based on smoking and health" imposed by state law "with respect to the advertising or promotion" of compliant cigarettes; New York's hypothetical Special COVID Warning Act of 2022 requires a sticker reading "Smoking increases the risk of hospitalization and death from COVID-19," and Philip Morris refuses (slide 14).

The Foie Gras hypo: California Health & Safety Code section 25491 bars force-feeding birds to enlarge their livers; MCABG Poultry, a Stockton producer, uses gavage to make foie gras and sues for declaratory judgment that the California law is preempted by the Federal Poultry Products Inspection Act, which preempts state requirements within the scope of the federal scheme but allows recordkeeping requirements (slide 15). The deck flags two later-course questions: whether the federal court has jurisdiction, and whether California could insulate itself from federal proceedings by creating a private $10,000-per-goose civil cause of action (slide 15) -- the SB 8 design from the Ex Parte Young lecture.

The Burger Control Act hypo: Congress caps "burger" fat at 25%; New York caps at 15% (preempted?); Texas requires at least 20% fat for non-beef burgers and at least 30% fat for beef burgers (preempted?); the deck closes by noting that physical conflict preemption is VERY rare (slide 16).

## Hypotheticals and Class Discussion

**Rail Safety Hypo:** Cara is injured by a freight train traveling 20 mph in fog (below the FRSA-permitted 25 mph) (slide 7). Her state negligence claim says the train should have slowed for fog, but FRSA regulations do not address weather-based speed restrictions. Is the state tort claim preempted? This requires analyzing whether FRSA's regulations occupy the field of train speed or only establish a partial regulatory floor, leaving room for state law to impose additional safety requirements in unaddressed circumstances.

**ERISA Benefits Hypo:** Wally seeks punitive and emotional distress damages under Texas law for an insurer's bad-faith underpayment of a medical claim (slide 8). ERISA permits suits but limits remedies (slide 8). Is the state bad-faith claim preempted? ERISA's "relates to" language is among the broadest preemption clauses in federal law (slide 6); the complete-preemption doctrine supports removal and substantive displacement of extra-contractual damages.

**Ukraine Sanctions Hypo:** Pennsylvania bars state procurement from entities on a "restricted purchase list" of Russian-linked businesses, after Congress enacted sanctions with executive discretion to waive (slide 11). Rusal had federal sanctions waived after agreeing to transparency measures (slide 11). The state law arguably stands as an obstacle to the federal scheme of calibrated, discretionary sanctions administration -- a strong field-plus-obstacle preemption argument in an area of traditionally federal foreign commerce and foreign affairs power.

**Cigarette Labels Hypo:** New York's COVID warning sticker requirement runs into FCLAA's preemption of "[n]o requirement or prohibition based on smoking and health … with respect to the advertising or promotion of any cigarettes" labeled in conformity with the chapter (slide 14). Whether a health warning on packaging is a "requirement based on smoking and health" and whether the sticker is "advertising or promotion" are the key interpretive questions.

## Key Takeaways

- The Supremacy Clause is the constitutional hook, but preemption is almost always a statutory interpretation problem: what did Congress intend to displace?
- The three-branch tree -- express, field, conflict (impossibility and obstacle) -- structures the analysis, but categories blend in practice.
- Physical impossibility conflict preemption is rare; most contested cases involve field preemption or obstacle conflict.
- Presumption against preemption is weaker in traditional federal regulatory domains (foreign affairs, maritime, banking) and where Congress has spoken expressly even if ambiguously.
- The scope of express preemption clauses (especially "relating to" in ERISA) drives outcomes; linguistic differences like "with respect to" versus "covering" signal different breadth.
