# Fourteenth Amendment Section 5

## Overview

<!-- ENRICH: Section 5 grants Congress "power to enforce, by appropriate legislation, the provisions of this article." The doctrinal question is the scope of that power: does Congress merely remedy 14A violations the courts have already identified, or may Congress legislatively interpret 14A and reach conduct the courts have not held unconstitutional? The post-Boerne "congruence and proportionality" test answers: remedial-only, with a record-based fit requirement. The Section 5 power is also a key abrogation hook — when Congress validly acts under Section 5, it can abrogate state sovereign immunity, which it cannot do under Article I (Seminole Tribe). Cross-link to Topics/Eleventh Amendment when that exists. -->

## Governing Rule

<!-- ENRICH: City of Boerne v Flores (1997) — Section 5 legislation must show "congruence and proportionality" between the injury to be prevented and the means adopted. Pure substantive interpretation by Congress (defining 14A's scope contrary to the Court) is invalid. The test has been applied case-by-case: Garrett (ADA Title I — fail), Hibbs (FMLA family-care — pass), Lane (ADA Title II as applied to court access — pass), Coleman (FMLA self-care — fail). -->
