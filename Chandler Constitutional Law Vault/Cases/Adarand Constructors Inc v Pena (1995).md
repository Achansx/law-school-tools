# Adarand Constructors Inc v Pena (1995)

## Memory Jogger

**The Federal Affirmative Action Case.** Adarand, a white-owned construction company, lost a federal subcontract to a Hispanic-owned company because a federal program gave financial incentives to prime contractors who hired "socially and economically disadvantaged" subcontractors (in practice, minority-owned firms). The key move: *Metro Broadcasting* (1990) had held that federal racial preferences needed only intermediate scrutiny. *Adarand* overrules *Metro Broadcasting* and extends strict scrutiny to all racial classifications by *any* governmental actor — federal, state, or local. Three principles: **skepticism** (racial preferences presumptively suspect), **consistency** (same standard for minority-benefiting and minority-burdening classifications), **congruence** (federal government bound by same standard as states under Fifth Amendment).

## Facts

- The Central Federal Lands Highway Division awarded a highway construction contract to Mountain Gravel & Construction Company as prime contractor
- Mountain Gravel solicited subcontract bids for guardrail work; Adarand Constructors submitted the low bid
- Mountain Gravel accepted a bid from Gonzales Construction Company instead — Gonzales was certified as a "small business controlled by socially and economically disadvantaged individuals" under the Small Business Act
- Federal regulations gave prime contractors a financial bonus for hiring such certified subcontractors; the bonus offset the additional cost of choosing Gonzales over lower-bidding Adarand
- Adarand challenged the regulatory program as a violation of the equal protection component of the Fifth Amendment Due Process Clause

## Procedural History

- District court: upheld program under *Metro Broadcasting*'s intermediate scrutiny standard
- Tenth Circuit: affirmed under *Metro Broadcasting*
- Supreme Court: reversed and remanded; overruled *Metro Broadcasting*; held strict scrutiny applies to all racial classifications including benign federal preferences

## Judicial Votes

- **5-4 decision** reversing the Tenth Circuit
- **Majority**: O'Connor (joined by Rehnquist CJ, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas) — strict scrutiny for all racial classifications
- **Concurrence**: Scalia — all racial preferences are unconstitutional regardless of benign intent; government can never act on the basis of race
- **Concurrence**: Thomas — separate concurrence emphasizing that government cannot treat individuals as members of racial groups for any purpose; racial paternalism is as demeaning as racial animus
- **Dissents**: Stevens (joined by Ginsburg), Souter (joined by Ginsburg, Breyer), Ginsburg (joined by Breyer) — *Metro Broadcasting* was correctly decided; meaningful difference between invidious discrimination and remedial preferences; strict scrutiny is inappropriate for programs designed to remedy historical exclusion

## Holding

All racial classifications imposed by any governmental actor — federal, state, or local — must be analyzed under strict scrutiny. To survive, a racial classification must be narrowly tailored to serve a compelling governmental interest. This rule is governed by three principles: (1) **skepticism** — any preference based on racial or ethnic criteria must necessarily receive a most searching examination; (2) **consistency** — the standard of review does not depend on the race of those benefited or burdened; (3) **congruence** — equal protection analysis under the Fifth Amendment (federal government) is the same as under the Fourteenth Amendment (states).

*Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC* (1990) is overruled to the extent it held that benign federal racial preferences require only intermediate scrutiny.

## Key Points

- **The rule**: all racial classifications by any government actor (federal, state, local) require strict scrutiny — compelling interest + narrow tailoring
- **Three principles**: (1) skepticism (racial criteria presumptively suspect); (2) consistency (same standard regardless of which race is burdened or benefited); (3) congruence (Fifth Amendment = Fourteenth Amendment for this purpose)
- **Metro Broadcasting overruled**: intermediate scrutiny for federal racial preferences is no longer valid law
- **Not per se invalid**: strict scrutiny is not fatal in fact — the majority remands to let the program try to survive (suggesting the door is not fully closed on all federal racial preferences)
- **Croson (1989)** precursor: struck down city minority set-aside program as failing strict scrutiny; *Adarand* aligns federal law with *Croson*
- **Post-Adarand trajectory**: *Grutter v. Bollinger* (2003) holds educational diversity is a compelling interest; *Students for Fair Admissions* (2023) overrules *Grutter* and effectively ends race-conscious admissions
