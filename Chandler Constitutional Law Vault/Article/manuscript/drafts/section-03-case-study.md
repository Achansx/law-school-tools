# III. Case Study: A Constitutional-Law Knowledge System

## A. The entry shell and the discovery surfaces

A student opens the deployed case study at https://constitutionallaw.netlify.app and meets a fixed shell: a top-nav listing Dashboard, Cases, Topics, Lectures, Recent, and About; a course brand row reading “Con Law I · Prof. Chandler · Spring 2026”; and a search-palette trigger labeled “Search cases, topics, doctrines” bound to ⌘K (Figure 3.1).[^1][^2] There is no login, no chat box, no account, and no personalization surface; every visitor sees the same shell, and every top-nav link routes inside it through a hash route rather than a server-rendered page load. Above the page level the site carries three discovery surfaces: the Recent tab lists pages the maintenance rotation has most recently touched; the ⌘K palette indexes case names, topic names, doctrine families, and lecture titles; and the four-family Topics grid plus the Cases area filter rail give the student a doctrine-first or area-first entry when the case name is not yet known.[^3] A reader who knows the doctrine, the case, or only the area can each start from the right surface and walk down to the page.

## B. Doctrine first: the Topic page walkthrough

A student who starts from doctrine opens the Topics index and lands on a four-family grid keyed to the doctrine_family enum Section V documents: Federalism, Separation of Powers, Individual Rights, and Justiciability (Figure 3.2).[^4][^5] Each family heading holds its constituent Topic pages as cards; clicking Federalism brings up the Commerce Clause card alongside Enumerated Powers, Necessary and Proper Clause, and the rest. Doctrine-first entry is the affordance the casebook table of contents does not give and the lecture-slide export does not give either; the deployed site presents the doctrines as a navigable space with their own destination pages.

The Commerce Clause Topic page renders the doctrine as a single synthesis (Figure 3.3).[^6][^7] An Overview names the constitutional text and the three structuring questions: what counts as commerce, what counts as among the states, and what regulation means. A Governing Rule block states each test as exam-ready prose with the controlling case attached inline, from *Gibbons* through *Jones & Laughlin Steel* and *Wickard* through *Lopez*, *Raich*, and *NFIB*. A Doctrinal Development section walks the arc in analytical order with wiki-link back-references at each case introduction; a Key Cases table links to thirteen briefs and lectures; a five-by-six comparison matrix isolates the *Wickard*-through-*NFIB* modern limit cases; a Hypothetical Applications block and a How-to-Spot-on-an-Exam framework close the doctrinal scaffolding; and a four-paragraph Critique surveys the progressive, originalist, institutional, and synthesis readings. Every test is attributed, every case in the arc is linkable, every paragraph is exam-relevant.

## C. From doctrine to case to lecture

A student following the Commerce Clause arc clicks the *Marbury v. Madison* (1803) wiki-link from a Topic page and lands on the populated Case brief (Figure 3.4).[^8][^9] The page carries the nine-section case-briefer standard Section V documents: Memory Jogger, Facts, Procedural History, Judicial Votes, Holding, Analysis, Hypothetical Applications, Critique, and Key Quotations. Above the prose the deployed shell renders a structured holding bar from the frontmatter, with the citation, court, doctrine_family, midpage_id, and an applied_in chain naming six downstream cases including *Martin v. Hunter’s Lessee*, *McCulloch*, *The Prize Cases*, and *City of Boerne*. The holding bar is the visible artifact of the frontmatter schema; the nine-section body is the artifact of the case-briefer template. The structure shows up at the surface a student reads, not only in the YAML the build script parses.

The third destination type is the Lecture summary. A student clicking the *Lecture: Marbury v. Madison* wiki-link from the *Marbury* Connections block lands on a page whose Professor Emphasis section compresses how Professor Chandler frames the case in class: foundational and historically contingent, with Marshall’s political genius in manufacturing a confrontation he was guaranteed to win, and with the dicta on right-remedy alignment, political question doctrine, and executive privilege carrying nearly as much downstream weight as the judicial-review holding itself.[^10] A Lecture Outline walks the session in the order it was delivered. The page captures the third leg of the case-doctrine-classroom triangle Section IV named as the casebook’s blind spot: a student who missed the session can recover the professor’s framing without listening to a recording, and the recovery is structured rather than verbatim.

Every populated page closes with a Connections block of wiki-links to every page touching the same doctrine, case family, or pedagogical thread. The *Marbury* Case brief’s block carries fifteen outbound links across four Topics, seven other Cases, and three Lectures, each rendered as a clickable badge (Figure 3.5).[^11][^12] The Connections block turns the three content folders into a navigable graph; the path from *Marbury* to *Martin v. Hunter’s Lessee* to *Cohens v. Virginia* traces the arc from 1803 through 1816 through 1821 without the student ever opening the Cases index.

## D. Hypotheticals and Midpage-verified quotations as the exam-prep payoff

Every populated Case brief and Topic page carries a Hypothetical Applications block in a fixed three-bucket form (Figure 3.6).[^13][^14] Two Same-Side hypotheticals come out the way the canonical case did; two Opposite-Side hypotheticals flip on a single distinguishing fact; and one Fence-Sitter is genuinely contested under the doctrine’s current state. The Commerce Clause page’s block runs five worked examples: a compelled-retirement-product statute that fails under *NFIB*’s activity-inactivity line; a school-zone firearm statute that fails under *Lopez* and *Morrison*; a *Darby*-with-jurisdictional-overlay statute that succeeds; a *Raich*-style comprehensive-scheme application that succeeds; and a state-internal appliance-recordkeeping statute that is genuinely uncertain because two doctrinal framings are both available.[^15] Each closes with a one-sentence Why naming the controlling doctrinal move. The hypotheticals are scoped to the doctrine the page is about; the three-bucket form is the surface-level proof that the prompting design Section VI describes produces pedagogically structured material rather than free-form exam noise.

Every Key Quotation on every populated Case brief is followed by a Midpage line-anchor URL that resolves to the cited line of the indexed opinion.[^16] The *Marbury* brief carries five linked quotations, and the Commerce Clause Topic page extends the same discipline into doctrinal prose: Chief Justice Roberts’s “The power to regulate commerce presupposes the existence of commercial activity to be regulated” passage in the Governing Rule block links to the *NFIB* line.[^17] A student or peer reviewer who clicks any pull-quote lands on the verified line of the source opinion without leaving the page. The Midpage line anchors are the visible discipline that Section VII’s iteration narrative produced from the *Prize Cases* failure story; the citation trail rides on the page the reader is already reading rather than on a follow-up query.

## E. What the experience is and what it is not

The user-experience contrast with a chatbot tutor is structural rather than rhetorical. A student using Sajja et al.’s 2023 curriculum-grounded intelligent assistant types a question into a chat surface and reads the system’s generated answer.[^18] A student using the deployed Constitutional Law I vault opens an index, opens a destination page, follows wiki-links across the Topic-Case-Lecture graph, and reads prose that the maintenance rotation produced and verified. Whatever the student would have asked a chatbot, the student instead finds, by navigating to where the relevant doctrine already lives. The trade-off is real: a chatbot can answer a question whose wording the student already knows; the static site requires the student to find the doctrinal neighborhood first. What transfers from the chatbot-tutor literature is the general value of curriculum-grounded retrieval for students seeking authoritative course material, not the conversational implementation surface; the vault adopts the curriculum-grounding insight in a static-publication form. The deployed artifact does not talk, listen, or respond; the student is the actor whose verbs carry the experience. Section XII develops the architectural-choice and risk-profile sides of the contrast, including the different hallucination surfaces and the different audit affordances that the static-site posture entails. Section III walks the user-experience side first so the reader sees what the case study is before the article argues why the choice was made.
