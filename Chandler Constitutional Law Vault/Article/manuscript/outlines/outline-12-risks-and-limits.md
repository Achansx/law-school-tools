## 12.A The lead anchor: hallucination findings and the practitioner-side consequence (160 words)

Evidence: the Magesh hallucination-findings card, first-use external citation with the Wiley DOI as the published-journal path and arXiv:2405.20362 as the pre-print path, URL liveness deferred to Cite per L-019 and L-022 with both candidate paths to verify per L-043. ## 12.B The architectural contrast and the discipline that organizes its prose (240 words)

- Claim: the workplan §4 line 104 directive names a static-site-is-safer-than-chatbot table as the second load-bearing element of Section XII; the architectural contrast enumerates that the case study is a reviewed static website where every page was professorially inspected before publication, while the chatbot-tutor alternative (Sajja-style curriculum-grounded systems) generates responses at runtime against a corpus and the user sees the generated text without prior professorial review, and the contrast lives along four axes: review timing (publication-time professorial pass versus runtime generation with no human in the loop), error surface (errors are stable, addressable, and citeable to a specific page versus errors are ephemeral and re-rolled each session), update mechanism (versioned commits to a Git repository versus opaque retrieval-and-generation against a corpus), and accountability surface (a named professor versus a system whose outputs no one signed). Inferential move: the four-axis contrast is the section's argumentative spine, and 12.B's first bullet does the work of stating the contrast directly so that subsequent bullets can name what the static architecture's own risks are without that naming reading as a retreat from the contrast. - Claim: the architectural contrast does not claim that the case study is risk-free; it claims the risk surface is different in kind and lives in different parts of the production pipeline, and the case study's own risk surface has four named features the section enumerates honestly: review-scale (every page passing professorial review is a discipline that scales linearly with vault size and therefore caps the rate at which the system can grow), lost-interactivity (a static site cannot answer a question the professor has not anticipated, which is a real pedagogical loss the chatbot architecture would address differently), structural-pedagogical-commitment (the doctrinal map the system encodes is the professor's commitment, and a student who internalizes the map without contesting it is not getting the live Socratic contest), and publication-bake-in (an error that survives review and ships to the public site is harder to retract than an ephemeral chatbot answer).
