## I.A The archive no one can open (200 words)

- Claim: every experienced doctrinal teacher carries a course in two places, the hard drive (syllabi, lecture decks, annotated cases, hypotheticals, exam rubrics) and the head (the accumulated judgment about where a class loses a doctrine and what question pulls it back), and almost none of the second kind survives outside the professor and the live classroom; the materials land in a learning-management folder as a flat list organized only by the filename string, and the ordering rationale rarely outlives the syllabus's bare list of assignments. Evidence: the hidden-archive card grounds the opening conceit in the workplan §2 corpus characterization; the concrete filesystem audit (61 PowerPoints, 66 PDFs, no nesting beyond filenames) lives in Section IV, which the intro's footnote [^1] reaches by `*see infra*` per the no-internal-cite rule. Inferential move: I.A states the problem at the altitude of a claim and hands the directory-listing proof forward to Section IV (The Input Corpus) and App. A, so the reader meets the "archive no one can open" image before Section IV quantifies it and Section II argues why ordinary infrastructure cannot solve it. [evidence: evidence-01-hidden-archive-flat-lms]

## I.B The claim (250 words)

- Claim: generative AI, working on structured source materials, Obsidian-style markdown notes, retrieval from verified legal sources, and iterative expert review, can convert a professor's scattered course materials into a navigable, cross-linked, student-facing course knowledge system; the constitutional-law case study is the demonstration and the method is the transferable contribution, and the essential feature is not automation but iteration under the control of a human expert who reviews, corrects, and signs off on every layer. Evidence: the thesis card transcribes the workplan §1 and PROJECT_PRIMER thesis sentence verbatim in shape (faithful transcription, not enlargement of scope). Inferential move: I.B states the thesis the whole article exists to support and hands the method-not-automation frame forward to every later section. [evidence: evidence-01-thesis-method-not-automation]
- Claim: review here is not a gloss applied at the end but is built into the process at defined checkpoints, where the professor inspects the drafted work, corrects what is wrong, and gates whether it advances or returns for another pass. Evidence: the professor-in-the-loop card grounds the checkpoint language in the workplan professor-as-scaffold posture and the PROJECT_PRIMER review gates; the operational mechanism (per-phase review, gates, the forced-finding Verify pass) is owned by Section VII, which footnote [^2] reaches by `*see infra*`. Inferential move: I.B asserts the checkpoint structure and hands the mechanism forward to Section VII (Iterative Improvement Under Professorial Control), so the reader takes the control claim on assertion now and sees it demonstrated there. [evidence: evidence-01-professor-in-the-loop-checkpoints]
- Claim: three negative framings fix the article's scope before the case study is shown: it is not the announcement of an AI teaching assistant and not a chatbot placed in front of students (the case study is a reviewed static website, and that architectural choice carries the later risk argument); and it makes no claim that students learned more, because it has not run the study that would license such a claim. Evidence: the recurring-sentence card fixes the canonical text and marks Section I as the first of exactly three approved placements (intro, the VII/VIII seam, the conclusion). ## I.C The case study, in one breath (170 words)

- Claim: the case study is concrete and public: a single professor's Constitutional Law I course, taught in one semester, became a live knowledge system of 198 pages (92 case briefs, 27 doctrinal topic pages, 79 lecture summaries), published at https://constitutionallaw.netlify.app and readable by anyone; each case page is written against the indexed judicial opinion rather than a paraphrase, so a quotation a student relies on traces to the controlling source, and the doctrinal scope is the structural constitution (judicial review, federalism, separation of powers, the commerce power, executive authority, justiciability, and the Reconstruction amendments). Evidence: the corpus-snapshot card carries the WebFetch (2026-05-23) liveness confirmation of the deployed site and its navigation; the page-count breakdown is owned by Sections IV and VIII and the source-quote discipline by Section III. Inferential move: I.C grounds the abstract claim in one verifiable artifact and hands the corpus counts forward to Section IV (footnote [^5], App. A snapshot) and the quotation discipline to Section III (footnote [^6]), fixing a bounded base case before Section X argues the method generalizes. [evidence: evidence-01-corpus-snapshot-and-live-url]

## I.D The conversation this enters (160 words)

- Claim: the article enters a conversation JLE has already begun: recent empirical work in its pages reports that AI assistance can help weaker students more than stronger ones on law-school examinations, with a potential equalizing effect, and that study shows serious inquiry at proportional rigor is possible without a controlled trial a single course cannot run; this article's contribution sits upstream of any classroom intervention, concerning the infrastructure a professor assembles before a student arrives and insisting a human expert remain in command of each step. Evidence: the Choi-Schwarcz card carries the verified citation (73 J. Legal Educ. 384 (2025)) and the WebFetch (2026-05-23) liveness confirmation of the JLE repository page; footnote [^8] reuses the verification and cross-references Section XI's proportional-rigor deployment per L-034. Inferential move: I.D fixes the field stakes-and-standard at intro altitude and hands the proportional-rigor posture forward to Section XI, framing the question as whether a professor can turn dispersed materials into a resource others can read, check, and build on. [evidence: evidence-01-choi-schwarcz-current-conversation]
