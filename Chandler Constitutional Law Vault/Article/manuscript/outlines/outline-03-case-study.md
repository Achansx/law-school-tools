# Section III. Case Study: A Constitutional-Law Knowledge System

Spine for the draft phase. Each H2 has a word budget; subtotal is 1,200. Each bullet states the claim, names the evidence, and signals the inferential move it carries for the rest of the article. Section III is the student-eye walkthrough of the deployed Con Law I vault: the workplan's Phase C item 2 directs the section to walk a reader from doctrine to case to hypothetical to cross-links the way a student would, and the bullets below trace exactly that arc. The section sits at drafting order 2 because Sections IV, V, and VIII have already established the input corpus, the typed intermediate vault, and the publication pipeline; Section III closes the IV-V-VIII concrete trio by showing what those layers produce at the surface a student or peer reviewer actually opens. Per L-026 the recurring sentence does not land in Section III; per L-031 the voice-unity sweep keeps the deployed artifact from carrying conversational verbs even in the chatbot-contrast subsection.

## 3.1 The entry shell and the discovery surfaces (180 words)

- Claim: the deployed site at https://constitutionallaw.netlify.app opens on a fixed top-nav (Dashboard, Cases, Topics, Lectures, Recent, About) with a ⌘K search-palette trigger and a course brand row reading "Con Law I · Prof. Chandler · Spring 2026," with no login, no chat box, and no personalization surface; every visitor sees the same shell, and every top-nav link routes inside the same shell via a hash route. Evidence: evidence-03-deployed-site-entry-shell. Inferential move: Section III opens by naming what a student first encounters before walking the doctrine-to-case path, and the shape of the entry surface (browse, not query) is the architectural fact Section VIII's pipeline argument and Section XII's risk argument both depend on; the absence of a chat box on the entry surface is the first visible artifact of the static-not-chatbot posture. [evidence: evidence-03-deployed-site-entry-shell]
- Claim: above the page level the deployed site carries three discovery surfaces, the Recent tab listing pages the maintenance rotation has most recently touched, the ⌘K search palette indexing case names, topic names, doctrine families, and lecture titles, and the four-family Topics grid plus the Cases area filter rail; a student who knows the doctrine, the case, or only the area can each start from the right surface and drill down. Evidence: evidence-03-recent-tab-and-search-palette. Inferential move: the discovery surfaces are the operational answer to a student who would otherwise type a question at a chatbot ("what is the Commerce Clause test?") — the static site lets the student find the page that already carries the answer rather than generate a fresh paraphrase, and the Recent tab in particular ties the student-eye surface to the maintenance rotation Section VII describes. [evidence: evidence-03-recent-tab-and-search-palette]

## 3.2 Doctrine first: the Topic page walkthrough (250 words)

- Claim: a student opens the Topics index, lands on the four-family grid (Federalism, Separation of Powers, Individual Rights, Justiciability) keyed to the doctrine_family enum Section V documents, and clicks into Federalism to find the Commerce Clause card; this is the doctrine-first entry the workplan names as the canonical walkthrough opener. Evidence: evidence-03-cases-and-topics-indexes. Inferential move: doctrine-first entry is the affordance the casebook table of contents does not give a student and the lecture-slide deck does not give either — the deployed site presents the doctrines as a navigable space with their own destination pages, which sets up the Section X generalization argument that the same shape works for any course whose doctrines can be enumerated. [evidence: evidence-03-cases-and-topics-indexes]
- Claim: the Commerce Clause Topic page renders the doctrine as a single synthesis: an Overview naming the constitutional text and the three structuring questions, a Governing Rule block stating each test as exam-ready prose with case attribution inline (Gibbons, Jones and Laughlin Steel, Wickard, Lopez, Raich, NFIB), a Doctrinal Development section walking the arc in analytical order with case back-links, a Key Cases table, a five-by-six comparison matrix on the modern limit cases, a Hypothetical Applications block, a How-to-Spot-on-an-Exam framework, and a four-paragraph Critique surveying the progressive, originalist, institutional, and synthesis readings. Evidence: evidence-03-topic-page-walkthrough. Inferential move: the Topic page is the synthesis form the vault produces that prose alone could not — every test is attributed, every case in the arc is linkable, every paragraph is exam-relevant — and the page's structure pays off in Section VI's prompting argument (the synthesis form is what the prompting design produces) and in Section V's schema argument (the form is what the schema enforces). [evidence: evidence-03-topic-page-walkthrough]

## 3.3 From doctrine to case to lecture (300 words)

- Claim: a student following the Commerce Clause arc clicks the *Marbury v. Madison* (1803) wiki-link from a Topic page and lands on a populated Case brief carrying the 9-section case-briefer standard Section V documents — Memory Jogger, Facts, Procedural History, Judicial Votes, Holding, Analysis, Hypothetical Applications, Critique, Key Quotations — with the frontmatter holding-bar block (citation 5 U.S. 137, year 1803, court, doctrine_family, midpage_id, applied_in chain of six downstream cases) rendered as a structured banner above the prose. Evidence: evidence-03-case-brief-walkthrough. Inferential move: the Case brief is the second destination type; the holding bar is the visible artifact of the frontmatter schema Section V describes, and the 9-section body is the artifact of the case-briefer template; together they prove that the structure Section V documents shows up at the surface a student reads, not only in the YAML a build script parses. [evidence: evidence-03-case-brief-walkthrough]
- Claim: the third destination type is the Lecture summary; a student who clicks the *Lecture: Marbury v Madison - Judicial Review* wiki-link from the Marbury Case brief's Connections block lands on a page whose Professor Emphasis section compresses how Professor Chandler frames *Marbury* in class (foundational and historically contingent, Marshall's political genius, the dicta on right-remedy alignment and political question doctrine and executive privilege carrying nearly as much downstream weight as the judicial-review holding), followed by a Lecture Outline walking the session in the order it was delivered. Evidence: evidence-03-lecture-page-walkthrough. Inferential move: the Lecture summary captures the third leg of the case-doctrine-classroom triangle that Section IV named as the casebook's blind spot — a student who missed a class session can recover the professor's framing without listening to a recording, and the recovery is structured rather than verbatim, which is the affordance Section II will argue ordinary course infrastructure cannot deliver. [evidence: evidence-03-lecture-page-walkthrough]
- Claim: every populated page closes with a Connections block of wiki-links to every other page touching the same doctrine, case family, or pedagogical thread; the Marbury Case brief's block carries fifteen outbound wiki-links across four Topics, seven other Cases, and three Lectures, each rendered as a clickable badge in the deployed shell. Evidence: evidence-03-wikilink-graph-navigation. Inferential move: the Connections block is what turns the three content folders into a navigable graph rather than a flat hyperlinked corpus, and the path Marbury → Martin v. Hunter's Lessee → Cohens v. Virginia traces the doctrinal arc from 1803 through 1816 through 1821 without the student ever opening the Cases index; the graph is the reading-path affordance Section X carries into the generalization argument. [evidence: evidence-03-wikilink-graph-navigation]

## 3.4 Hypotheticals and Midpage-verified quotations as the exam-prep payoff (270 words)

- Claim: every populated Case brief and Topic page carries a Hypothetical Applications block in a fixed three-bucket form — two Same-Side hypos that come out the way the canonical case did, two Opposite-Side hypos that flip on a single distinguishing fact, and one Fence-Sitter that is genuinely contested under the doctrine's current state — scoped to the doctrine the page is about, with each hypo closing in a one-sentence Why that names the controlling doctrinal move; the Commerce Clause Topic page's block runs the broccoli-retirement-product, school-zone-firearm, Darby-with-jurisdictional-overlay, Raich-style comprehensive-scheme, and intrastate-recordkeeping examples. Evidence: evidence-03-hypothetical-applications-affordance. Inferential move: the three-bucket form is the article's signature exam-prep artifact and the surface-level proof that the prompting design Section VI describes produces pedagogically structured material rather than free-form exam noise; the affordance distinguishes the deployed vault from a casebook supplement (fewer worked examples per doctrine) and from a lecture-slide export (no hypotheticals at all), which is the comparative claim Section II will land. [evidence: evidence-03-hypothetical-applications-affordance]
- Claim: every Key Quotation on every populated Case brief is followed by a Midpage line-anchor URL that resolves to the cited line of the indexed opinion; the Marbury brief carries five linked quotations and the Commerce Clause Topic page extends the same discipline into doctrinal prose, so that a student or a peer reviewer who clicks any pull-quote lands on the verified line of the source opinion without leaving the page. Evidence: evidence-03-midpage-verified-quotations. Inferential move: the Midpage line anchors are the visible discipline that the Prize Cases failure story (Section VII's anchor) produced — every quotation on the prose surface is verifiable in one click, and the citation trail is on the page the reader is already reading rather than retrieved by a follow-up query; this is the reviewer-grade surface the article points back to in Section VII's iteration argument and in Section XI's evaluation framing. [evidence: evidence-03-midpage-verified-quotations]

## 3.5 What the experience is and what it is not (200 words)

- Claim: the user-experience contrast with a chatbot tutor is structural rather than rhetorical: a student using Sajja et al.'s 2023 curriculum-grounded intelligent assistant types a question into a chat surface and reads a generated answer, while a student using the deployed Con Law I vault opens an index, opens a destination page, follows wiki-links across the Topic-Case-Lecture graph, and reads prose the rotation phases produced and verified; whatever the student would have asked a chatbot, the student instead finds, by navigating to where the relevant doctrine already lives. Evidence: evidence-03-static-not-chatbot-experience. Inferential move: Section III closes by naming the trade-off honestly (the chatbot answers a question whose wording the student already knows; the static site requires the student to find the doctrinal neighborhood first) and by pointing forward to Section XII for the architectural-choice and risk-profile sides of the contrast; per L-003 and L-031 the prose stays static-site-framed throughout — the deployed artifact does not talk, listen, or respond, and the student is the actor whose verbs carry the experience. [evidence: evidence-03-static-not-chatbot-experience]
