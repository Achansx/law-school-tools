## 14.A The archive, opened: the method as the contribution (130 words)

- Claim: the article opened with a law professor's hidden archive that no one, the professor included, could open as a whole (Section I), and it closes by stating what opened it: not automation, but a method in which generative AI, structured source materials, typed markdown notes, retrieval from verified legal sources, and iterative expert review convert that dispersed archive into a navigable, cross-linked, student-facing course knowledge system. The contribution is the method, not the artifact: the constitutional-law system is the demonstration, and the transferable result is the replicable process other doctrinal courses with similar archives can follow. Evidence: the hidden-archive framing card and the thesis-as-method-not-automation card establish the Section I bookend; the method-not-artifact contribution card (Section X) establishes that the transferable claim is the process, not the constitutional-law pages. Per L-046 these are existing sibling cards; PI-097 flags the owed `evidence-14-method-as-contribution-recap` card. Per L-027 the 198-page corpus is cross-referenced to Section IV / I rather than re-quoted as a fresh count. Inferential move: 14.A closes the Section I "archive no one can open" frame by naming the opener (the method), restating the thesis for the last time, and handing the "what the demonstration showed" question to 14.B. ## 14.B What the case study showed, and what it did not claim (140 words)

- Claim: the case study showed a tractable pipeline from source files to typed notes to a deployed, reviewed static site, governed by a per-phase rotation, a running lessons memory, and a forced-finding verification pass, all gated by a professor who reviewed each layer before it advanced; and the article is as careful about what it did not claim as about what it showed. It does not claim students learned more, because it did not run the study that would license that claim (Section XI); it does not claim universal transfer, treating constitutional law as one well-suited base case rather than proof every doctrinal course transfers cleanly (Section X); and it does not present a chatbot, because the case study is a reviewed static website whose risk surface sits at build-time professorial review rather than runtime generation (Section XII). Evidence: the six-phase-rotation and professorial-control-mechanism cards establish the loop-under-review; the not-a-claim-students-learned-more card (Section XI) and the method-not-learning-outcomes-limit card (Section X) ground the two disclaimers; the static-vs-chatbot risk-architecture card (Section XII) grounds the architectural disclaimer. Per L-003 the static-not-chatbot framing holds; per L-024 the generalization limit cross-references Section X rather than re-arguing it. PI-097 flags the owed `evidence-14-disclaimers-recap` card. Inferential move: 14.B converts the recap into a credibility claim (the article earns trust by bounding itself) and hands the "what remains" question to 14.C, where professorial judgment and the forward invitation close the piece.
