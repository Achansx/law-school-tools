# Section II. Why Ordinary Course Infrastructure Is Not Enough

Spine for the draft phase. Each H2 has a word budget; subtotal is 700. Each bullet states the claim, names the evidence card, and signals the inferential move it carries for the rest of the article. Section II sits at drafting order 7, after the IV-V-VIII concrete trio (the input corpus, the typed vault, and the publication pipeline), after Section III (the student-eye walkthrough), and after Sections VI, VII, and IX (the prompting, iteration, and cost-and-labor arguments) have established what the case study actually is. The section's job is the higher-altitude framing claim: the inherited inventory of casebook, syllabus, slide deck, and LMS post is not a course knowledge system, and was never designed to be one. The article extends rather than rejects that inventory. Per L-002 and L-026 the recurring sentence does not land in Section II even though Section II's argument is the closest paraphrase of it of any section in the article; per L-031 and L-040 the voice-unity sweep checks for personifying verbs attached to the deployed artifact and for the static-artifact-as-substitute-for-live-pedagogy slip; per L-027 any numeric claim routes to its owning section (cost figures to Section IX, audit counts to App. A) rather than being fabricated here.

## 2.1 The long-range frame: a static method on shifting ground (140 words)

- Claim: Edward Rubin's 2007 Vanderbilt Law Review article argues that legal education has used essentially the same Langdellian model since the 1870s despite enormous changes in law, practice, theory, and pedagogy, and the persistence of that model is the long-range fact any pedagogical proposal must reckon with rather than try to overturn. Evidence: the Rubin annotation framing of casebook persistence as a stable inheritance rather than a deficiency to remove. Inferential move: Section II opens by accepting the casebook and the case method as inherited infrastructure rather than positioning the article as a critique of either, which sets up the article's central move at the close of the section (the contribution is the layer the casebook never had, not a replacement for the casebook) and which keeps the article's posture aligned with the JLE pedagogical-reform line Abrams represents at 2.5. [evidence: evidence-02-rubin-langdell-static-method]

## 2.2 What ordinary course infrastructure produces: an inventory (150 words)

- Claim: the starting state of the Constitutional Law I vault, before any of the pipeline ran, was a flat folder with two hundred sixty-plus files exported from a law-school course management system — sixty-one PowerPoint lecture decks, sixty-six PDFs of case readings, merged reading packets, practice exams, teaching guides — organized only by filename, with no structural index, no doctrinal map, no cross-reference between a lecture and the cases it covered, and no synthesis layer above the inventory. Evidence: the archive blog-draft starting-state description, paired at Cite time with the public-source anchor that App. A carries. Inferential move: Section II uses this concrete operational image to make the central observation that the inherited inventory IS what ordinary course infrastructure produces; the point is not that any tool in the inventory is wrong, but that the inventory is the starting point rather than the system, which sets up the rest of the section's affordance-gap argument. [evidence: evidence-02-flat-folder-lms-starting-point]
- Claim: the May 2026 progress snapshot of the case study records one hundred ninety-eight pages of structured prose alongside one hundred fifty-four still-unprocessed source materials, and the coexistence of the two corpora at one snapshot date proves that turning the inventory into the system is real, separate work rather than a property the inventory has by itself. Evidence: the email-to-chandler-progress.md snapshot lines anchoring the post-Ingest counterpart to the flat-folder starting state. Inferential move: the snapshot pair grounds the inventory-to-system transformation as work the article will account for honestly — Section IX owns the labor accounting per L-027, and Section II carries only the qualitative claim that the transformation is real — without overclaiming any efficiency or productivity figure per L-001. [evidence: evidence-02-slides-and-readings-disconnect]

## 2.3 The performed teaching ordinary infrastructure does not capture (140 words)

- Claim: Elizabeth Mertz's ethnographic study of first-year Contracts classrooms (Oxford University Press 2007) records that much of what a professor actually teaches happens in real-time classroom interaction — Socratic exchange, framing moves, hedges, the moment-by-moment shaping of how a case gets read — and never lands in any artifact a student or a successor instructor can return to later. Evidence: the Mertz card's compressed observation about the performed component of teaching. Inferential move: Section II deploys Mertz briefly (the workplan's directive of restraint) to name what the casebook, syllabus, LMS, and slide deck collectively do not capture, which sets up Section V's typed-schema argument (the Lecture summary page captures the professor's framing in structured form as a supplement to the live session) and Section XII's risk-profile contrast (a static synthesis surface does not substitute for the live event; it records and feeds it). [evidence: evidence-02-mertz-performed-teaching]
- Claim: a second affordance the inherited inventory does not produce is a verification surface that compares the prose a student reads to the indexed opinion that controls; the Prize Cases modernized-PDF failure (a silent update to archaic phrasing that survived multiple automated passes until an adversarial AI-analysis read caught it) illustrates the gap, and the full narrative belongs to Section VII as the article's anchor iteration story. Evidence: the modernized-PDF anchor failure brief reference, used at the one-or-two-sentence forward-pointer altitude per L-038. Inferential move: Section II grounds the affordance-gap claim in a concrete failure mode rather than leaving it abstract, then forward-references Section VII for the full Prize Cases narrative; this keeps Section VII's anchor function intact while letting Section II carry a non-abstract grounding point. [evidence: evidence-02-modernized-pdf-silent-update-as-infrastructure-failure]

## 2.4 The synthesis and graph affordances ordinary infrastructure does not produce (180 words)

- Claim: a third affordance gap is the doctrinal-synthesis view a student needs at exam time; the May 2026 stages email describes the Topic page form as the synthesis surface of the vault — a page that pulls every case brief and lecture summary touching a doctrine into a single view carrying the governing rule, doctrinal development from earliest controlling case to most recent, a Key Cases comparison table, hypotheticals, an exam-spotting framework, and a critique block — and Commerce Clause is the running example the email names. Evidence: the Topic-pages-as-synthesis-form stages-email excerpt. Inferential move: the Topic page is the synthesis surface a casebook (which organizes by reading order), a syllabus (which organizes by week and learning objective), and an LMS (which organizes by post date and folder) collectively do not produce, and Section II names the gap that Section III walks at the user-experience altitude and Section V documents at the schema altitude. [evidence: evidence-02-topic-pages-as-synthesis-form]
- Claim: a fourth affordance gap is a navigable graph that lets a student move laterally from a doctrine to the cases that develop it to the lecture summaries that frame it and back; the deployed vault carries an explicit wiki-link grammar (Topic to Case, Case to citing case, Lecture to Case and Topic) and the navigation surface that grammar produces is the doctrinal map a casebook, syllabus, LMS, or slide deck does not produce because none of them is a graph. Evidence: the workplan §2 highlight on structured schemas and wiki-link conventions kept at the why-this-matters altitude per L-027. Inferential move: Section II names the graph affordance at the architectural level while leaving the walking-the-graph work to Section III and the conventions-and-schema work to Section V; the affordance also carries forward into Section X's generalization argument that the same graph shape works for any course whose doctrines and cases can be enumerated. [evidence: evidence-02-wikilink-graph-affordance]

## 2.5 Positioning within the JLE pedagogical-reform line (90 words)

- Claim: the article sits within an established JLE line of pedagogical-reform pieces that adapt rather than reject inherited teaching forms, of which Jamie R. Abrams's 2015 piece on reframing the Socratic method is the recent representative, and the course knowledge system extends the casebook, the syllabus, and the live classroom rather than displacing any of them. Evidence: the Abrams JLE 2015 annotation as a tone-and-positioning reference. Inferential move: Section II closes with a single positioning citation that signals venue fit and clarifies the article's stance against any reading of the affordance-gap argument as a critique of the casebook or the case method, which is the rhetorical posture every later section inherits and which Section XIII's institutional argument relies on to keep the proposal incremental rather than confrontational. [evidence: evidence-02-abrams-jle-pedagogical-reform]
