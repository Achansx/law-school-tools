## 9.B Hosting cost: bounded under normal operation, blocked when credits exhaust (130 words)

- Claim: the deployed-site hosting bill is a two-state architectural property rather than a single number; the preferred deploy path is Netlify CLI direct upload from the scheduled task, which does not consume Netlify build minutes because the build runs in the local sandbox and only the produced assets cross the wire, and the Netlify MCP fallback path is explicitly demoted in DEPLOY.md because it does consume build minutes, so under normal operation the deploy phase lands at zero marginal hosting cost on the free tier; the honest hedge is the credit-block pattern documented in vault LESSONS.md and the three logged credit-block events on 2026-05-07 at 18:41Z, 19:11Z, and 19:42Z, where the free-tier ceiling reasserts itself as an HTTP 403 (`Account credit usage exceeded`) and the RUNBOOK guard skips the fast_deploy POST after three consecutive same-type failures until human action restores credits or upgrades the tier. Evidence: evidence-09-netlify-cli-zero-build-minutes; evidence-09-credit-block-as-external-cost-signal. Inferential move: the two-state framing is the article's structural answer to a reviewer who asks whether free-tier Netlify is honestly free for a production deploy, and the credit-block hedge is what keeps Section IX from overclaiming the zero-marginal-cost case; the same two-state framing supports PI-018's Section VIII forward-reference because Section VIII names the pipeline shape qualitatively and Section IX carries the bounded-versus-blocked numeric line, with the credit-restoration step explicitly named as human action rather than a phase the system can take itself. [evidence: evidence-09-netlify-cli-zero-build-minutes; evidence-09-credit-block-as-external-cost-signal]
