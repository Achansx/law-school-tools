## 7.1 The Karpathy loop, domesticated (200 words)

- Claim: Andrej Karpathy's autoresearch release on March 7, 2026 is a roughly six-hundred-thirty-line Python script that delegates the modify-run-evaluate-keep-or-discard loop to an LLM agent and ran fifty experiments overnight to surface a learning-rate improvement; the structural insight the article imports is the loop architecture (per-phase rubric, lessons file as durable memory, rolling scorecard, forced-finding adversarial pass), and the implementation surface the article does not import is the autonomous-agent setting that runs unsupervised against a measurable scientific objective. Evidence: evidence-07-karpathy-autoresearch-concept. Inferential move: this is the framing claim that licenses the rest of Section VII to walk the four-piece adaptation (rotation, Verify, audit trail, professorial control) and that pre-emptively answers the JLE reader's reasonable suspicion that the article is importing Silicon Valley benchmark culture into legal pedagogy; per L-024 the Cite phase or the Draft phase (per the Section 06 Qian precedent) lands the transfer clause in body prose naming the autonomous-agent-versus-professor-in-the-loop distinction and the measurable-versus-non-measurable-objective distinction; per L-001 the prose carries only the verified fifty-experiments figure and never asserts the seven-hundred-experiments or eleven-percent-efficiency-gain numbers the professor flagged as Silicon Valley lore. [evidence: evidence-07-karpathy-autoresearch-concept]

## 7.2 One phase per tick: the rotation as the loop (180 words)

- Claim: the vault runs a six-phase rotation (Ingest, Lint, Enrich, Expand, Synthesize, Verify, then repeat) with one phase per scheduled-task run, so the rotation itself is the loop rather than a single autonomous pass that touches every phase every cycle; each phase has a documented procedure in `RUNBOOK.md` and a separate weighted rubric in `rubric/<phase>.md`, and the per-phase split is the article's structural answer to the autonomous-agent setting Karpathy's autoresearch occupies. Evidence: evidence-07-six-phase-rotation. Inferential move: the one-phase-per-tick commitment is what makes each pass small enough for a professor to inspect on its own terms and lets phase-specific quality differ instead of being averaged into a single per-pass score; the architectural property sets up 7.3's forced-finding Verify argument (because Verify gets its own focused pass), 7.4's audit-trail argument (because each phase writes its own scorecard line), and 7.5's professorial-control argument (because the scope-selection step on each phase can prefer professorial input over the rotation's own next-in-queue work). [evidence: evidence-07-six-phase-rotation]

## 7.3 The forced-finding Verify pass: catching what other phases let pass (300 words)

- Claim: the Verify phase runs three hostile personas (Staleness Auditor, Contradiction Hunter, Template Enforcer), each required to return at least one concrete finding per run; a persona that genuinely finds nothing records `persona-produced-nothing` as its own finding so the absence of findings is auditable rather than invisible, and a lead-rotation table plus a per-persona focus-mutation table both keyed off `state.verify_run_count` reshape the lead-and-focus pairing every pass so the same persona on two consecutive runs does not default to its safest playbook. Evidence: evidence-07-forced-finding-verify. Inferential move: the forced-finding requirement is what converts Verify from a phase that confirms things look fine into a phase that actually catches what other phases missed; the article's case for the loop turns on Verify doing real work, which is also the structural feature the article's own writing system reproduces in `Article/PERSONAS.md` and `Article/rubric/verify.md`. [evidence: evidence-07-forced-finding-verify]
- Claim: the Prize Cases / Grier quotation correction is the canonical iteration-loop story, and it is concrete enough to anchor the section per the workplan's drafting brief for Section VII; the vault said `never formally declared...by its character` (the professor's modernized PDF silently rewrote archaic phrasing), Justice Grier actually wrote `never solemnly declared...by its accidents`, Midpage `findInOpinion` returned zero results when asked to verify and the zero was logged as an OCR artifact and left alone, the corrupted quotation ran through Ingest, Lint, two Enrich passes, and Expand before a Verify persona specifically told not to accept zero results as an answer switched from `findInOpinion` to `analyzeOpinion` and Midpage returned Grier's actual language, and the correction was then grep-driven back across the vault to catch the stale copy on the War Powers topic page. Evidence: evidence-07-prize-cases-grier-quote. Inferential move: one worked example shows the loop catching its own error after several phases let it pass and documents the methodological move (analyze, not just search) the LESSONS file then encoded as a durable amendment that 7.4 picks up; per L-004 the article's own writing system applies the same `analyzeOpinion` discipline to the Grier quotation it reports, making the article reflexively consistent with the method it describes. [evidence: evidence-07-prize-cases-grier-quote]
- Claim: a second iteration story sits alongside the Prize Cases correction and shows the same loop catching a different type of error: the Youngstown Judicial Votes section first read that Frankfurter, Douglas, Jackson, and Burton all `joined` Black's majority, a defensible shorthand but misleading for a law student given that Youngstown's six-justice majority produced six different opinions with two justices joining the opinion and also writing separately and three concurring in the judgment only; the page sat partially wrong through multiple cycles because no earlier phase was specifically looking for the mischaracterization, and the Contradiction Hunter persona on a focus-area-rotation pass dedicated to concurrence-versus-majority mischaracterization triggered an `analyzeOpinion` re-check of Frankfurter's actual language and produced the rewrite. Evidence: evidence-07-youngstown-joined-vs-concurred. Inferential move: two cases of the loop catching what the prior phases let pass make a stronger empirical argument than one and broaden the failure-mode coverage from quotation-text errors (Prize Cases) to doctrinal-characterization errors (Youngstown), which is the kind of failure mode JLE readers will recognize from their own teaching and the kind the bare per-phase rubric is least likely to catch without the focus-area rotation. [evidence: evidence-07-youngstown-joined-vs-concurred]

## 7.4 Loop memory: LESSONS as durable record, scorecard and narrative as audit trail (250 words)

- Claim: `LESSONS.md` is the loop's running memory, with a fixed-schema entry (Rule, Why, How to apply, optional dated Seen footer) capped at thirty-five entries, an out-of-rotation Consolidate sweep that merges duplicates and prunes superseded entries when the file passes the cap or accumulates three lesson-candidate items, and tiered loading so Ingest, Enrich, Expand, and Synthesize read the file because they generate content while Lint and Verify skip it because the corrections that govern content generation are different from the corrections that govern structural checks. Evidence: evidence-07-lessons-as-loop-memory. Inferential move: the lessons file is what answers a reader who asks whether the loop is actually improving or just running, and the same architecture reappears in `Article/LESSONS.md` (cap fifty, drop lowest-impact-on-add), which makes the article's own method legible by the standard it describes; per L-027 the per-phase token-loading economics are described qualitatively (loaded only by content-editing phases; tiered by phase type) without quoting per-phase dollar or wall-clock figures, which belong to Section IX's cost-and-labor table. [evidence: evidence-07-lessons-as-loop-memory]
- Claim: the token-problem rubric-split story shows the loop reshaping its own infrastructure rather than just its content; early runs loaded one large `RUBRIC.md` covering all six phases, the full `LESSONS.md`, and the entire build narrative every pass, so a Lint run that only cared about structural formatting was burning context reading Enrich and Verify criteria it would never use, and a no-op Enrich run was still loading the full lessons file and scoring itself before concluding it had nothing to do; the fix was tiered loading (rubric split into per-phase files, lessons skipped by Lint and Verify, build narrative rotated monthly, no-op phases stopped being scored), and a secondary fix fell out of the same change because the shared rubric had let one phase game a criterion in a way that made its own score look good while making another phase's job harder. Evidence: evidence-07-token-problem-rubric-split. Inferential move: the story documents the loop modifying its own architecture rather than just its content, which is the second kind of iteration the section argues for (alongside the content-error iteration 7.3 documents), and the same per-phase-rubric-only principle is the article's own L-007. [evidence: evidence-07-token-problem-rubric-split]
- Claim: the run-by-run scorecard at `.run-scores.jsonl` and the rolling `BUILD_NARRATIVE_YYYY-MM.md` are the loop's audit trail, with the scorecard holding one JSON line per run containing the active phase, the weighted criteria scores, the red-flag list, and the timestamp, and the narrative holding a two-to-four-sentence prose entry per run describing what the phase actually did and what was learned; the rubric's per-criterion weights and red-flag thresholds define what counts as a flagged-for-human-review run, and the two artifacts together let a reviewer reconstruct the loop's state at any past moment. Evidence: evidence-07-scorecard-and-build-narrative. Inferential move: the audit trail is what makes the loop reviewable rather than opaque, which is the condition the article needs for a JLE-publishable methodology rather than a Silicon Valley demo, and the same paired artifacts reappear in `Article/.article-scores.jsonl` and `Article/BUILD_NARRATIVE_YYYY-MM.md`. [evidence: evidence-07-scorecard-and-build-narrative]

## 7.5 Professorial control: the page-feedback intake and the staging triplet (170 words)

- Claim: the vault's answer to professorial control is the page-feedback intake at Step 0 of every scheduled-task run, which pulls inbound comments from the deployed-site page-feedback form before the phase dispatcher selects the active phase, triages each submission into one of six routes (trivial fix, structural, analytical, cross-reference, topic-level, verification), and logs a typed `professor-feedback` pending issue routed to the phase that owns the fix; trivial fixes land on the same run, structural and substantive feedback waits for the phase that owns the fix to rotate around, and the phase's scope-selection step must prefer pages with open `professor-feedback` issues routed to that phase, so reviewer comments shape what the rotation works on next. Evidence: evidence-07-professorial-control-mechanism. Inferential move: the page-feedback intake is the architectural feature that distinguishes the vault's loop from a Karpathy-style autonomous loop, and the staging triplet (Trump v. United States as skeleton, the Rehnquist Court Commerce Clause lecture as mid-cycle, Gonzales v. Raich as full enrich) is the observable evidence that the loop produces different artifacts at different stages of the rotation rather than a single one-shot output; the section closes with the recurring sentence: The system did not replace professorial judgment; it made that judgment reusable, inspectable, and publishable. [evidence: evidence-07-professorial-control-mechanism; evidence-07-three-stage-iteration-pair]
