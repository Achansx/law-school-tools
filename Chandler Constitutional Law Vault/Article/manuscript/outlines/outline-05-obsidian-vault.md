# Section V. Building the Obsidian Vault

Spine for the draft phase. Each H2 has a word budget; subtotal is 1,100. Each bullet states the claim, names the evidence, and signals the inferential move it carries for the rest of the article. Section V is the intermediate layer: it sits between the raw LMS export Section IV inventoried and the deployed static site Section VIII describes, and it is the artifact Section VII's iteration record operates on. The argument is that the vault's structure (folder taxonomy, frontmatter schemas, fixed page templates, wiki-link grammar, locked enums) does retrieval and rendering work that prose alone could not, and that the structure is reactive — it evolved in response to specific failures rather than being designed top-down.

## 5.1 From flat folder to typed schema (200 words)

- Claim: the vault organizes its content into three primary folders, Cases, Topics, and Lectures, each backed by a canonical template in Templates and a per-page YAML frontmatter schema, with Source Materials as a read-only input folder, rubric for per-phase scoring, and archive for rotated narratives. Evidence: evidence-05-vault-folder-structure. Inferential move: this is the schema that translates the heterogeneous LMS surface Section IV described into the typed intermediate layer Section VIII later converts into a website; the three content folders correspond to three distinct units of legal-pedagogy work (a decided case, a doctrine, a class session) and that typing is what makes the vault behave as a schema rather than a folder of prose. [evidence: evidence-05-vault-folder-structure]
- Claim: the vault enforces a strict three-way invariant: filename equals H1 equals the frontmatter case_name, topic_name, or lecture_title, with no periods and "and" not "&". Evidence: evidence-05-filename-and-h1-discipline. Inferential move: this is the smallest structural commitment that produces vault-wide consistency the downstream tooling relies on; wiki-links resolve mechanically rather than via heuristic normalization, which is what lets Section VIII describe the build script as a deterministic compiler and not a fuzzy matcher. [evidence: evidence-05-filename-and-h1-discipline]

## 5.2 The Case Brief as the canonical page type (250 words)

- Claim: every Cases page opens with a roughly 30-field YAML frontmatter block covering doctrinal labels, citation metadata, authority lineage, Midpage verification anchors, and a structured holding-bar block the deployed site promotes into a banner above the prose. Evidence: evidence-05-frontmatter-schemas. Inferential move: this is the load-bearing surface for Section VII's iteration-under-professorial-control argument because Enrich and Verify operate on typed fields rather than re-parsing prose every run; the schema is what makes the loop tractable. [evidence: evidence-05-frontmatter-schemas]
- Claim: every Cases page follows a fixed H2 sequence inherited from Templates/Case Brief.md: Memory Jogger, Facts, Procedural History, Judicial Votes, Holding, Analysis (with Majority required and Concurrence and Dissent optional), Hypothetical Applications (Same-Side, Opposite-Side, Fence-Sitter, totaling five), Critique, Key Quotations, Key Points, Connections, Sources. Evidence: evidence-05-case-brief-nine-section-standard. Inferential move: the Hypothetical Applications block is the pedagogically distinctive piece, and it is the typed surface Section VI's prompting design then leverages to generate exam-style hypotheticals on a known shape rather than free-form text; the Critique block's progressive-and-textualist requirement is what keeps Section VI from looking like content-generation alone. [evidence: evidence-05-case-brief-nine-section-standard]
- Claim: a populated brief shows what the schema produces once Enrich has filled the empty Ingest-time fields. The Marbury v Madison (1803) brief carries midpage_id 84759, a canonical Midpage URL, cited_by 3,995 pulled from CourtListener, an applied_in list naming six downstream Supreme Court cases (Martin v Hunter's Lessee, McCulloch v Maryland, The Prize Cases, United States v Rahimi, Cohens v Virginia, City of Boerne v Flores), the locked four-bucket value doctrine_family: Separation of Powers, and the full holding-bar block. Evidence: evidence-05-machine-readable-frontmatter-values. Inferential move: the build script renders a holding bar, a citation count, and an authority lineage display directly off these typed fields without parsing the prose, which is the concrete payoff Section VIII later cashes out as a website that students browse. [evidence: evidence-05-machine-readable-frontmatter-values]

## 5.3 Topic pages as the synthesis layer (250 words)

- Claim: the Topic page is the synthesis form, where the vault stops being a folder of briefs and starts being a knowledge system; a single Topic pulls every Case brief and Lecture summary touching a doctrine into one page with an Overview, a Governing Rule block in exam-ready language, a Doctrinal Development section that walks the cases in analytical order, a Key Cases table, five Hypothetical Applications, an exam-spotting framework, and a Critique. Evidence: evidence-05-topic-synthesis-form. Inferential move: this is where the wiki starts earning its keep for outlining and exam review, and it is the layer Section X's generalization argument pivots on — the synthesis form transfers to any doctrinal subject; the case-brief template by itself does not carry the argumentative reuse. [evidence: evidence-05-topic-synthesis-form]
- Claim: the Judicial Review topic is the canonical example: frontmatter key_cases lists Marbury (1803), Martin v Hunter's Lessee (1816), The Prize Cases (1863), and Youngstown (1952); the source_files attribution lists three Cases pages and two Lectures pages directly consulted; the prose stitches them into a single doctrinal narrative with `[[Cases/...|...]]` back-links at every case introduction. Evidence: evidence-05-topic-synthesis-form. Inferential move: this demonstrates the provenance-from-prose-back-to-source chain that distinguishes the vault from a chatbot that confabulates its sources, and it is the architectural contrast Section XII later draws between the case study and chatbot-tutor systems. [evidence: evidence-05-topic-synthesis-form]
- Claim: the wiki-link grammar is small and fixed: cross-page links take the form `[[folder/filename|Display Text]]`; tags are lowercase-hyphenated and placed at file end before a closing `---`; every page carries a source_files frontmatter list in lockstep with a `## Sources` footer that the Lint phase enforces. Evidence: evidence-05-wikilink-conventions. Inferential move: the deployed site renders the graph view, runs search across pages, and surfaces cross-references because the link grammar is mechanical and the provenance trail is verifiable; this is what makes Section VIII's claim that the vault publishes itself an architectural consequence rather than a marketing line. [evidence: evidence-05-wikilink-conventions]

## 5.4 Schema doing work prose alone cannot (200 words)

- Claim: the doctrine_family field on the Case Brief template carries exactly four values — Federalism, Separation of Powers, Individual Rights, Justiciability — and the same enum is used on Topic Page templates' area and family fields. Evidence: evidence-05-doctrine-family-enum. Inferential move: the build script reads this one typed field to drive which color column a case or topic appears under in the deployed site's four-family grid and the holding-bar accent on case pages; this is the smallest unit of "schema doing work the prose alone could not" and the cleanest example for Section V's central claim. [evidence: evidence-05-doctrine-family-enum]
- Claim: the enum is locked: RUNBOOK requires a page that does not fit any existing value to be realigned to the nearest canonical value rather than the enum widened in place; widening is a vault-wide change requiring an explicit template-guide edit, a RUNBOOK update, and a BUILD_NARRATIVE note. Evidence: evidence-05-doctrine-family-enum (RUNBOOK enum realignment policy paragraph). Inferential move: this is the structural commitment that prevents per-page drift, and Section VII later picks it up as exactly the kind of explicit constraint a human author imposes on an LLM-assisted system to keep iteration coherent. [evidence: evidence-05-doctrine-family-enum]
- Claim: external research supports the architectural claim. The Dong et al. KG-RAG study reported a knowledge-graph-augmented retrieval system outperforming pure semantic RAG in a controlled comparison (76 students, mean scores 6.37 versus 4.71, p<0.001, Cohen's d=0.86), and the Peng et al. GraphRAG survey defines the canonical workflow (graph-based indexing, graph-guided retrieval, graph-enhanced generation) the vault implements manually. Evidence: evidence-05-kg-rag-anchor; evidence-05-graph-rag-positioning. Inferential move: the article describes the Obsidian vault as a manually maintained, professor-supervised cousin of GraphRAG, where the human-curated link graph and typed frontmatter do the retrieval work pure semantic similarity cannot; Section VI uses this footing for the prompting-as-pedagogy argument, and Section X uses it for the generalization argument. [evidence: evidence-05-kg-rag-anchor; evidence: evidence-05-graph-rag-positioning]

## 5.5 The structure is reactive, not designed top-down (200 words)

- Claim: the per-phase rubric architecture and tiered file loading were forced by a token-cost problem the system actually encountered. Early runs loaded a single large rubric covering all six phases, the full LESSONS file, and the entire build narrative every run; a Lint run that cared only about structural formatting was burning context reading Enrich and Verify criteria it would never use, and a no-op Enrich run was scoring itself against a rubric that did not apply. The fix was structural: split the rubric into six per-phase files, skip LESSONS for phases that do not need it, rotate the build narrative monthly, and log no-op phases as one-line entries rather than scoring them. Evidence: evidence-05-token-problem-and-per-phase-rubrics. Inferential move: this is the lived demonstration that the vault's schema is not architectural decoration; the structure was forced by what made the system work, and Section VII picks up this thread to show how lessons feed forward into the loop. [evidence: evidence-05-token-problem-and-per-phase-rubrics]
- Claim: the area-enum realignment policy is the schema-side companion to the rubric split. The Judicial Review topic was initially assigned `area: "Federal Judicial Power"`, which is not one of the four canonical doctrine_family values; the page was realigned to Separation of Powers rather than the enum widened in place, and RUNBOOK now codifies that any future drift triggers a vault-wide change with explicit edits and a narrative note. Evidence: evidence-05-doctrine-family-enum (RUNBOOK realignment paragraph). Inferential move: this is the second concrete iteration story Section V needs so the reactive-schema claim does not rest on a single anchor; PI-011 is provisionally addressed by writing this bullet into the spine, and Draft phase carries the prose. [evidence: evidence-05-doctrine-family-enum; addresses PI-011]
- Claim: the vault's schema is therefore not a designed-up-front object; it is a sequence of structural commitments each forced by a specific failure mode and preserved as a rule the next run obeys, with LESSONS.md as the running memory. Evidence: evidence-05-token-problem-and-per-phase-rubrics; the LESSONS lineage cited in PROJECT_PRIMER. Inferential move: this is the formulation that makes Section VII's iteration-under-professorial-control claim earned rather than asserted; by the time the reader reaches Section VII, the vault's schema has already been shown to be a captured record of professorial judgment expressed as structural rules. [evidence: evidence-05-token-problem-and-per-phase-rubrics]
