## 10.A The case study as an instance (180 words)

- Claim: the constitutional-law knowledge system the article has described is one instance of a method, and the method is the part meant to travel; the case study is bounded on purpose (a single professor's Constitutional Law I course, one semester, the structural constitution rather than the full subject), because a bounded base case makes the generalization claim testable rather than rhetorical. The contribution is the method, not the finished artifact: a method can be run again on a different corpus, while a finished site can only be admired or copied. Evidence: the method-not-artifact card grounds the load-bearing framing (workplan §4 names Section X with the Introduction as the two load-bearing rhetorical sections; workplan §10 locates the essential feature in iteration under professorial control, not automation; PROJECT_PRIMER's thesis puts the broader contribution in the method). Inferential move: 10.A draws the bound-base-case framing from Section IV (course scope) and Section I (the method thesis) and sets up 10.B's question of what, concretely, travels from this instance to another. [evidence: evidence-10-method-not-artifact-contribution]

## 10.B What transfers is the structure, not the content (220 words)

- Claim: what generalizes first is the shape of the schema, not its constitutional-law content. The vault sorts every page into three first-class units, one for a decided case, one for a doctrine, and one for a class session, and the article argues these three units recur across doctrinal courses. Evidence: the three-content-types card supplies the verified base fact (the case-study vault's three-folder taxonomy, source-verified against PROJECT_PRIMER) and explicitly marks the "recur in nearly every doctrinal course" step as the article's own inference, not an evidenced fact (PI-085 flags the confident phrasing as overclaim). Inferential move: draws the schema description from Section V and sets up the form-versus-values distinction the next two bullets sharpen. [evidence: evidence-10-three-content-types-recur]
- Claim: the nine-section case brief the vault enforces is the standard law-school brief, not a constitutional-law artifact, so a torts or contracts course would brief its cases in the same Facts, Holding, and Analysis shape; only the cases and doctrines filling the slots differ. Evidence: the nine-section-brief card (source-verified against the Case Brief template and PROJECT_PRIMER's "9-section case-briefer standard" label) supports the form-is-course-agnostic half and does not claim the doctrinal content transfers. Inferential move: draws the template fact from Section V and supplies the defensible, narrow half of the transfer claim. [evidence: evidence-10-nine-section-brief-course-agnostic]
- Claim: only the values are particular. The typed doctrine_family field is structural and course-agnostic in form, while its four values (Federalism, Separation of Powers, Individual Rights, Justiciability) are particular to constitutional law; a torts vault keeps the typed enum and replaces the values with negligence, intentional torts, and strict liability. Evidence: the doctrine-family-values card (source-verified against the Case Brief template and the RUNBOOK enum-realignment policy) is the sharpest concrete instance of structure transferring while content does not, the single typed field that promotes to a visible color column. Inferential move: draws the enum fact from Section V and closes 10.B on the form-transfers-values-do-not distinction that 10.D will bound. [evidence: evidence-10-doctrine-family-values-are-particular]

## 10.C What transfers is the loop and its discipline (180 words)

- Claim: the production process generalizes alongside the schema. The six-phase rotation, the lessons file that carries memory between runs, the adversarial review pass that must return a finding, and the professorial checkpoints are features of the method, not of constitutional law; what makes the method safe to hand to a different faculty member is that the professor remains the gating actor at every phase. The verified-source discipline transfers with one substitution, a case-law course checking each quotation against the indexed opinion while a statute-heavy course would check against the code and its history. The architectural commitment carries too: the case study is a reviewed static website rather than a system that generates answers at the moment students ask. Evidence: the loop-and-discipline card (source-verified against RUNBOOK: dispatcher protocol, Verify three-persona procedure, gate stop-conditions) grounds the loop facts and flags the statute-substitution tooling as an open gap (PI-089), since the case study's verification depends on a case-law-specific indexed-source service. Inferential move: draws the loop from Section VII and the verification discipline from Section III, holds the L-003 static-not-chatbot line (forward to Section XII) and the L-040 professor-as-gating-actor framing, and sets up the limits 10.D names. [evidence: evidence-10-loop-and-discipline-transfer]

## 10.D The limits of the claim (200 words)

- Claim: the claim has edges, and naming them is part of making it honestly. The method assumes a course organized around tagged doctrines and leading cases; a rules-and-procedure course (Civil Procedure) or a heavily statutory course (Tax) fits that assumption less cleanly, because the load-bearing unit is a rule or code section rather than a leading case, and the three-folder taxonomy would need re-typing first. The general ground for thinking the method still reaches such courses is that doctrinal courses share a learnable structure of rule, exception, and application; whether that structure maps as cleanly onto a procedure course is a question the section raises rather than settles. Evidence: the Usman card (Saadiq F. Usman, Making Legal Education Stick, Legal Writing Inst. 2018, URL verified live) supports the "learnable rule, exception, and application structure" half only, and explicitly does NOT support the bridge to "maps onto a small set of note types," which is the article's own design inference (PI-090); PI-086 separately flags the optimistic-bridge framing as asserted against the section's own hedge. Inferential move: draws the cognitive-structure ground from Usman, bounds 10.B's confident transfer claim, and sets up the second-instance test in 10.E. [evidence: evidence-10-usman-cognitive-structure]
- Claim: one limit is firm. What generalizes is the method, not a measured improvement in how students learn, because the article has not run the study that would license a learning-outcomes claim in this course, let alone in another. Evidence: the method-not-learning-outcomes card ties this firm limit back to the article-wide disclaimer Section XI owns (source-verified against the PROJECT_PRIMER thesis and the workplan §1 "not a claim that students learned more" bullet). Inferential move: a generalization section is exactly where a reader expects an efficacy claim to creep in, so 10.D restates the disclaimer and forward-references Section XI rather than relying on Section XI alone. [evidence: evidence-10-method-not-learning-outcomes-limit]

## 10.E From a method to a second instance (120 words)

- Claim: the strongest test of a method is whether a second person can run it, and this article reports one instance rather than two, so the generalization claim is an invitation as much as a result. The corpus, the schema, and the rotation are described in enough detail that a colleague teaching a different doctrinal course could attempt the same build and report where it held and where it broke. Evidence: the loop-and-discipline card grounds the "described in enough detail to re-run" claim (the method's mechanisms are RUNBOOK-specified, not constitutional-law-specific), and the method-not-artifact card grounds the invitation framing (a method re-runs; a site only copies). Inferential move: closes Section X by reframing generalization as an invitation and hands the "what would count as the method having worked, in this course or in another" question directly to Section XI (Evaluation). [evidence: evidence-10-loop-and-discipline-transfer]
