The May 2026 stages email to Professor Chandler describes the Topic page form explicitly as the synthesis surface of the vault: a Topic pulls together every case brief and lecture that touches a doctrine into a single page carrying the governing rule, doctrinal development from the earliest controlling case to the most recent, a Key Cases comparison table, five hypotheticals, an exam-spotting guide, and a critique block. Section II uses the Topic page as the operational image of what ordinary course infrastructure does not produce. A casebook organizes by reading order. A syllabus organizes by week and learning objective. An LMS organizes by post date and folder. None of those produces the doctrinal-synthesis view a student actually needs at exam time, which is the view the Topic page produces. The article's claim in Section II is not that the inherited tools are useless; it is that the synthesis surface is missing from the inherited inventory and has to be built separately. The Commerce Clause Topic page (named in the email) is the running example.
