At institutional scale the professor's role as the gating actor becomes an explicit governance question: who reviews each page, who maintains the vault, and who is accountable for the public artifact. The reviewed-static-website architecture is what makes the governance posture inspectable. Every page ships from a versioned commit and is accountable to a named professor who reviewed it before publication. The accountability distinction the article should draw is to a named pre-publication reviewer rather than to auditability as such: a runtime system can log its generated responses, so the load-bearing contrast is that the chain of responsibility on a reviewed static site runs to a named professor who inspected the page before it shipped, an accountability a runtime system's generated responses do not carry (this framing supports the PI-093 narrowing).

The recognition question follows but stays open. Whether a versioned, citable, publicly served course artifact counts as a scholarly output, as teaching infrastructure, or as both is the recognition question an institution must decide for review and promotion; the article names that question without pre-deciding it (this framing supports the PI-094 recast). Per L-044 the recognition hedge is paired with the architectural-implication sentence: the inspectable, versioned static artifact is precisely what makes either form of recognition assessable, because a reviewer can read what was published and trace who reviewed it.
