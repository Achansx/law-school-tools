The corpus the article describes is bounded by what was uploaded to Canvas, not by what the syllabus assigned. Ten foundational SCOTUS cases the syllabus references have wiki briefs (built from Midpage's analyzeOpinion API) but no corresponding source file on disk: Cohens v Virginia (1821), United States v Cruikshank (1876), Wickard v Filburn (1942), Adamson v California (1947), Baker v Carr (1962), USDA v Moreno (1973), South Dakota v Dole (1987), United States v Lopez (1995), United States v Morrison (2000), and Hamdi v Rumsfeld (2004). Students presumably read these in the assigned casebook rather than from a course-issued PDF. Section 04 should disclose this boundary; Section II can use it to motivate the gap that the course knowledge system fills.

Exact source quote, MISSING_SOURCE_MATERIALS.md paragraph 2:

> The course wiki at https://constitutionallaw.netlify.app currently has 131 case briefs. 10 of them lack a corresponding PDF or slide deck in the Canvas LMS export under Source Materials/. The wiki briefs themselves exist, they were created from Midpage's analyzeOpinion API, but the deployed site can't offer readers a download link to the underlying opinion or any course-issued reading because no file is on disk.
