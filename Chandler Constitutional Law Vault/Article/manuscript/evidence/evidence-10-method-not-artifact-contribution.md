The article's transferable contribution is the method, not the single deployed artifact, and Section X is where that claim is made explicit. The workplan marks the generalization section as load-bearing rhetoric rather than description, and its closing one-sentence framing locates the essential feature in iteration under professorial control rather than in automation. PROJECT_PRIMER's thesis sentence carries the same emphasis: the Con Law I vault is the case study, and the broader contribution is the method. This grounds X.A's framing that a method can be run again on a different corpus while a finished site can only be admired or copied. The card supports the method-over-artifact framing only; the separate question of how much of the schema actually transfers to another course is the article's own argument in X.B and is logged as an open overclaim risk under PI-085, not settled here.

> This article describes a method for turning a law professor's scattered course archive into a structured, cross-linked, student-facing course knowledge system. The method uses generative AI, but its essential feature is not automation. Its essential feature is iteration under professorial control.
