The Topic page is the synthesis form. A single Topic pulls every Case brief and Lecture summary touching a doctrine into one page: an Overview, a Governing Rule block stating the test in exam-ready language, a Doctrinal Development section that walks the cases in analytical order (foundational case first, then refinements, then limitations) with a back-link to each full brief, a Key Cases table, five Hypothetical Applications, an exam-spotting framework, and a Critique. The Judicial Review topic is the canonical example: its frontmatter `key_cases` list cites Marbury (1803), Martin v Hunter's Lessee (1816), The Prize Cases (1863), and Youngstown (1952); its `source_files` attribution lists three Cases pages and two Lectures pages it directly consulted; the prose stitches them into a single doctrinal narrative with `[[Cases/...|...]]` back-links at every case introduction.

Exact source quote, `Chandler Constitutional Law Vault/email-to-chandler-stages.md` paragraph 4:

Commerce Clause pulls together every case brief and lecture that touches commerce: governing rule, doctrinal development from Gibbons through Raich, a Key Cases table, five hypotheticals, how to spot on an exam, and critique. That is where the wiki starts earning its keep for outlining and exam review.
