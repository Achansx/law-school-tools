The Topic-Case-Lecture wiki-link graph is the architectural affordance that ordinary course infrastructure does not produce. A casebook lets a student turn a page; a syllabus lets a student locate a week; an LMS lets a student open a file in a folder; a slide deck lets a student step through a sequence. None of those is a graph, and none of them lets the student move laterally from a doctrine to the cases that develop it to the lecture summaries that frame it and back. The deployed Constitutional Law I vault carries an explicit wiki-link grammar — Topic pages link to Cases and Lectures, Cases link back to Topics and forward to citing cases, Lectures link to the Cases and Topics they covered — and the navigation surface that grammar produces is the synthesis surface a student uses for outlining and exam review. Section II's higher-altitude claim is that the graph exists at all and is not in the inherited inventory of casebook, syllabus, slides, and LMS posts. Section III walks the graph at the user-experience level; Section V documents the wiki-link conventions; Section II's job is to name the affordance gap.
