Edward Rubin's 2007 Vanderbilt Law Review article argues that legal education has used essentially the same Langdellian model since the 1870s despite enormous changes in law, practice, theory, and pedagogy. The article does not propose abandoning the casebook or the case method. It treats the persistence of Langdell's structure as the long-range fact about doctrinal teaching that any pedagogical proposal must reckon with. Section II of the JLE article uses Rubin as the long-range frame for the claim that ordinary course infrastructure (casebook, syllabus, lecture slides, LMS posts) is not enough: not because it is wrong, but because nothing in that inherited inventory was designed to capture the structural and doctrinal connections a professor builds in class and in office hours. The course knowledge system is not a critique of the casebook; it is the kind of infrastructure the casebook never had and was never designed to be.
