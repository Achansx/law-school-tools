The method's labor is professor-led but institutionally supported, and naming that split is the institutional implication of Section XIII.B. The cost-and-labor accounting in Section IX establishes the qualitative tractability: the build is tractable for a single professor working with a scheduled assistant, and the deployment runs on near-zero-cost direct-upload static hosting with no build minutes. Per L-027 the actual dollar figures, person-hour totals, and infrastructure numbers live in Section IX's master cost table and are not repeated in Section XIII; Section XIII names only the qualitative property and cross-references Section IX for the numbers.

Sustaining the work across a faculty and across course offerings is a different matter, because it implicates institutional roles the individual professor cannot carry alone. The library stewards an open-resource catalog, instructional design partners on the schema and its pedagogy, and information technology hosts and version-controls the build pipeline. The role mapping is the article's own institutional reading layered on top of Section IX's four labor categories (ingest, prompt tuning, review, debugging); it is qualitative, not a new cost line. A course knowledge system is therefore best read as faculty scholarship of teaching coupled with institutional stewardship, not as an unfunded individual burden.
