Section XII compares the case study to chatbot architectures and explains the architectural choice; every earlier section must keep the framing consistent. Section XI is the section where the static-versus-chatbot framing matters most for evaluation prose. The section's evaluation instruments (Likert survey, Netlify analytics, think-aloud sessions) measure use patterns and self-report on a reviewed static artifact. The evaluation literature most likely to be cited in Section XI (Bond et al. 2024 on AIHEd rigor, Sajja et al. 2023 on a curriculum-oriented chatbot assistant, Choi & Schwarcz 2025 on LLM exam assistance) is built on chatbot-tutor and LLM-assistance objects rather than on reviewed static websites. The drafting risk is that Section XI's prose silently adopts chatbot-tutor evaluation vocabulary — response quality, conversational coherence, tutor accuracy, engagement turns, dialogue-state tracking — when describing this article's evaluation, and the slip will be invisible to a reader who has not internalized the L-003 distinction.

Section XI's evaluation vocabulary should stay anchored on: navigation patterns, page-view distribution, search-term distribution, time on page, self-report on usefulness, self-report on trust, self-report on overreliance, think-aloud click sequences, think-aloud verbalization, and the qualitative methods-paper question of whether the artifact's structure makes professorial judgment legible to the student-reader. None of these is a chatbot-tutor measure; all of them are use-pattern and self-report measures on a reviewed static website.
