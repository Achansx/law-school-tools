A student using the Sajja et al. 2023 intelligent-assistant prototype interacts by typing a question into a chat surface; the system answers from a curriculum-grounded retrieval pipeline. A student using the deployed Con Law I vault at `https://constitutionallaw.netlify.app` interacts by browsing the top-nav (Dashboard, Cases, Topics, Lectures, Recent, About), opening an index, opening a destination page, following wiki-links across the Topic-Case-Lecture graph, and reading prose. The user-experience contrast is the through-line for Section III's framing of the case study: the deployed system answers no questions; it shows the structured prose the professor wrote (and the article's pipeline reviewed) and lets the student navigate it. Whatever the student would have asked a chatbot, the student instead finds, by following the wiki-link grammar from where they are now to where the relevant doctrine lives. The trade-off is real: a chatbot can answer a question whose wording the student already knows; the static site requires the student to find the doctrinal neighborhood first. Section XII develops the architectural-choice and risk-profile side of this contrast (different hallucination surface, different audit affordance, different review pipeline). Section III's job is to walk the user-experience side first so the reader understands what the case study is before Section XII argues why the choice was made.
