L-003 (`Article/LESSONS.md` impact-score-5 lesson) names Section XII explicitly: "Section XII compares it to chatbot architectures and explains the architectural choice." Section XII is therefore the structurally most-load-bearing site in the article for the static-versus-chatbot framing discipline. Every earlier section avoids slipping into chatbot-tutor framing for the case study so that Section XII can land the architectural contrast cleanly; Section XII itself faces the inverse risk, which is that the section will explicitly describe the chatbot-tutor architecture as the contrast object, and the description must remain a description of the contrast architecture rather than slipping into adopting that architecture's vocabulary for the case study's artifact.

The card pairs L-003 with L-031 (Polish voice-unity sweep includes a static-site-framing slip check, not only the explicit AI-tutor vocabulary check) and L-040 (live-pedagogy-versus-static-artifact slip check) as the three operational discipline rules Section XII's prose must satisfy. L-003 prohibits "AI tutor" and "chatbot" framing for the case study. L-031 extends the rule to verbs and metaphors that personify the static artifact (talks back, answers, responds, replies, knows, learns, adapts). L-040 extends the rule to sentences that position the static artifact as a substitute for a live pedagogical event the artifact merely supplements. Section XII faces all three risks at once because the chatbot-tutor contrast object is the section's explicit subject matter.

Section XII's drafting risk is asymmetric to earlier sections. At earlier sections the risk is silent vocabulary import. At Section XII the risk is mid-sentence vocabulary slippage: a sentence may open with "the chatbot-tutor architecture answers a student question by..." (correctly describing the contrast object) and then continue "...where the case study's architecture answers the same question by..." (incorrectly importing the answers verb to the case study's static artifact). The discipline rule is that any verb the section uses for the chatbot-tutor architecture must be revised to a non-personifying verb for the case study's architecture; the contrast must be rendered as the case study publishes a reviewed page that a student reads rather than as the case study answers the student's question with a published page.

The Section XII drafting vocabulary anchors on the right verbs for each architecture. The chatbot-tutor architecture: answers, responds, generates, retrieves, ranks, replies, converses, tracks state, adapts to context, personalizes to the user. The case study's static-website architecture: publishes, surfaces, indexes, links, displays, exposes, organizes, structures, reviews, audits. The two vocabularies do not interchange; Section XII's prose discipline keeps each architecture in its own vocabulary throughout the section.
