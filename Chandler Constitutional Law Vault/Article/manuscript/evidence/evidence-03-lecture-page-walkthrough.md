> Professor Chandler frames Marbury as simultaneously foundational and historically contingent -- a case that established judicial review not through a logically necessary argument, but through Marshall's political genius in manufacturing a confrontation he was guaranteed to win. The lecture stresses that Marbury matters nearly as much for its dicta (the right-remedy alignment, the political question doctrine, and the seeds of executive privilege) as for its core holding on judicial review. Students should watch for Marbury "exceptions" throughout the semester: moments when later courts nominally honor Marbury while quietly undercutting its rule-of-law premise.
