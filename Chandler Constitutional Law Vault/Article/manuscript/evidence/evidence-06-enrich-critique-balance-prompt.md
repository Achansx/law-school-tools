The Enrich phase prompt for the Critique section requires at least one progressive angle and one originalist or textualist angle per enriched brief, each grounded in either a published critique or a clearly signposted independent argument. The Enrich rubric's critique_balance criterion enforces the requirement mechanically: a brief missing one angle scores 3, missing both scores 1. The two-angle requirement is what prevents the prompt from collapsing into whichever interpretive frame the model would default to in the absence of explicit instruction. For a constitutional law setting the choice matters: a critique that treated only progressive readings of doctrine would systematically misrepresent the field, and a critique that treated only originalist or textualist readings would do the same in the opposite direction. The pedagogical commitment is that students must learn to read opinions from positions other than their own, and the prompt encodes that commitment at the structural level rather than relying on the model to remember it.

>    - **Critique**: at least one progressive angle and one originalist/textualist angle, each grounded in a published critique or a clearly signposted independent argument. Identify logical weaknesses, competing values, and open questions.
