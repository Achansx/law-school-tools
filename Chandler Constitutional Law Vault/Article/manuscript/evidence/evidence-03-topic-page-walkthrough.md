A student opens the Commerce Clause Topic page after navigating from the four-family Topics grid into Federalism. The page renders the doctrine as a single synthesis: an Overview that names the constitutional text and the three structuring questions (what counts as commerce, what counts as among the states, what regulation means); a Governing Rule block stating each test as exam-ready prose with the case attribution inline (Functional Definition from Gibbons (1824); Substantial-Effects Test from NLRB v Jones and Laughlin Steel (1937); Aggregation Principle from Wickard (1942); Three-Category Framework from Lopez (1995); Comprehensive-Scheme Exception from Raich (2005); Activity-Inactivity Line from NFIB (2012)); a Doctrinal Development section that walks the arc in analytical order with `[[Cases/...|case]]` back-links at each introduction; a Key Cases table with thirteen rows linking out to either the full brief or the lecture; a five-by-six comparison matrix on the Wickard-through-NFIB modern limit cases; a five-hypothetical Hypothetical Applications block split into Same-Side, Opposite-Side, and Fence-Sitter examples; a How-to-Spot-on-an-Exam framework; and a four-paragraph Critique surveying the progressive, originalist, institutional, and synthesis readings. The Connections block at the bottom links upward to Enumerated Powers, sideways to Necessary and Proper Clause and Implied Powers, and downward to every constituent Case brief and Lecture. Section III's walkthrough treats this page as the proof that doctrine-first entry is a real student affordance, not a list of files.
