Jamie R. Abrams's *Reframing the Socratic Method*, 64 J. Legal Educ. 562 (2015), is a recent JLE piece on adapting traditional law-school pedagogy rather than rejecting it. The workplan deploys Abrams in Section II for two reasons: as a tone reference (JLE welcomes structured arguments for pedagogical reform written in the practitioner-scholarly register the article uses throughout), and as a citation for the proposition that the journal's own pages already contain a line of pedagogical-reform pieces the article situates itself within. The rhetorical posture Section II takes toward ordinary course infrastructure parallels the posture Abrams takes toward Socratic teaching: the inherited form is not the problem; what is missing is the structure around it that would make professorial judgment reusable. The course knowledge system extends rather than replaces the casebook, the syllabus, and the live classroom, just as Abrams's reframing extends rather than replaces Socratic pedagogy.
