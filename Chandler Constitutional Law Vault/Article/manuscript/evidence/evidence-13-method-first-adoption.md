Adoption is opt-in and method-first. An institution adopts the method rather than the constitutional-law artifact: the typed schema, the verified-source discipline, the professorial gating, and the phase rotation. This adoption is consistent with Section X's claim that the structure transfers while its constitutional-law content does not. The institutional implication is a modest, replicable infrastructure investment offered as an invitation rather than a mandate, and what an institution must decide is whether to extend that invitation, a choice the conclusion takes up.

The "modest, replicable" framing carries a standing qualification: it assumes the institutional stewardship of Section XIII.B (library, instructional design, information technology), and that stewardship is itself unevenly distributed across institutions, so the framing overstates for under-resourced schools. The qualification is owned by the risk catalogue and cross-referenced rather than resolved here (see `evidence-13-institutional-resource-asymmetry.md` and PI-095).

Per L-002 and L-026 the recurring sentence ("The system did not replace professorial judgment; it made that judgment reusable, inspectable, and publishable.") does not land in Section XIII; 13.D hands the generalization-as-institutional-choice thread forward to Section XIV's conclusion, the third and final placement.

Source-record excerpt, `Chandler Constitutional Law Vault/Article/PROJECT_PRIMER.md` line 34 (the method-not-artifact thesis the adoption claim rests on):

> the Chandler Con Law I vault is the case study, and the broader contribution is the method.

Cross-reference: `evidence-10-method-not-artifact-contribution.md` and `evidence-10-loop-and-discipline-transfer.md` carry the Section X generalization basis (the method, schema, verified-source discipline, and phase rotation transfer across doctrinal courses while the constitutional-law content does not). Section XIII does not re-argue generalization; it cites Section X and applies the conclusion to the institutional-adoption choice.
