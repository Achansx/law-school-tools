Workplan §6 (open decisions for the professor) line 171 names student work as input as the second of six open decisions. The substantive directive is to confirm whether the professor's past exam answers, office-hours notes, or LMS posts were used as training input to the vault. The directive states the binary contingency directly: if yes, the article needs a privacy section in Section IV and Section XII; if no, the article should say so explicitly in Section XII. Either path produces a Section XII risk-catalogue entry, and the entry's content depends on the professor's answer to the input-status question.

The most likely status (which the article will need the professor to confirm before drafting) is that no student work was used as training input. The vault construction described in Sections IV and V works from course-issued materials (lecture decks, professor-prepared opinion PDFs, merged reading packets, practice exams, teaching guides) and from public judicial opinions, not from student submissions. The Section XII privacy-framing entry on the explicit-no path is a short paragraph that names the discipline the article followed and that flags student work as an input the method is capable of using but did not use in the case study and should not use without the corresponding privacy infrastructure (FERPA-compliant data handling, IRB review when appropriate, consent and anonymization protocols, separate data-retention rules).

The Section XII risk catalogue has a separate but related entry for the deployed site's analytics surface: the public site uses PostHog with Do Not Track respected and session recording disabled (already disclosed at Section VIII footnote [^37]/locally [^5], the Section VIII analytics footnote). The analytics surface is a privacy surface even when no student work was used as training input, because it records use-pattern data from the actual student readers of the deployed pages. Section XII's risk catalogue covers both the input-side privacy framing (whether student work was used as training input) and the use-side privacy framing (what the deployed site's analytics collects from current readers), with the two surfaces clearly separated in prose so a reader does not conflate them.
