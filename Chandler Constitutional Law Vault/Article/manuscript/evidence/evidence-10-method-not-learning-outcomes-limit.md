Section X.D states one firm limit on the generalization claim: what travels is the method, not a measured improvement in how students learn, because the article has not run the study that would license a learning-outcomes claim in this course, let alone in another. This is the article-wide disclaimer that Section XI owns and that the thesis itself encodes: the contribution is a method claim, not a treatment-effect claim on student performance (see evidence-11-not-a-claim-students-learned-more, source-verified against the PROJECT_PRIMER thesis sentence and the workplan §1 disclaimer bullet). A generalization section is the place a reader most expects an efficacy claim to slip in, so X.D restates the limit rather than relying on Section XI alone. The card keeps the generalization honest: the method is what another course could adopt and report on, and any learning benefit remains unmeasured here and would remain unmeasured there absent a study.
