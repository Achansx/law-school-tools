A second iteration story sits alongside the Prize Cases / Grier correction. The Youngstown Judicial Votes section first read that Frankfurter, Douglas, Jackson, and Burton all "joined" Black's majority — a defensible shorthand but misleading for a law student. Youngstown's six-justice majority produced six different opinions and no one fully endorsed Black's categorical rule: Frankfurter and Douglas joined the opinion and also wrote separately; Jackson, Burton, and Clark concurred in the judgment only. The first version of the brief had the shorthand; the page sat that way for several cycles because no earlier phase was specifically looking for the distinction. The Verify rotation's Contradiction Hunter persona on a focus area dedicated to concurrence-vs-majority mischaracterization triggered an `analyzeOpinion` re-check of Frankfurter's actual language and produced the rewrite.
