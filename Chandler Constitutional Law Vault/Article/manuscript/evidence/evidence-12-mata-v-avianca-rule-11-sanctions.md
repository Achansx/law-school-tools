Mata v. Avianca, Inc., 678 F. Supp. 3d 443 (S.D.N.Y. 2023), is Judge P. Kevin Castel's Rule 11 sanctions order against attorney Steven A. Schwartz for filing a brief containing six fabricated case citations generated by ChatGPT. The case has become the canonical real-world example of generative-AI hallucination producing professional-responsibility consequences for the lawyer who relied on the tool. Section XII deploys Mata as a one-sentence anchor that the hallucination rates Magesh measures are not a hypothetical concern and that the practitioner-side cost of a no-verify habit is documented and sanctionable. The same sentence carries a teaching-connection move per the workplan: students who internalize a no-verify habit through their AI tools will repeat the Schwartz error once they reach practice, which is one of the reasons a course knowledge system that habituates verification at the case-brief level matters pedagogically.
