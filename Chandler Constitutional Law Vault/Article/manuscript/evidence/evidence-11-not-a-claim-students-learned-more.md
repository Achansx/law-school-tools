The article's PROJECT_PRIMER (line 34) and workplan §1 (line 24) both carry the disclaimer that the article does not claim that students learned more from the case study. Section XI is the section in which the disclaimer becomes operational. The article's thesis is that generative AI combined with structured source materials, Obsidian-style markdown notes, retrieval from verified legal sources, and iterative expert review can convert a law professor's dispersed course archive into a navigable, replicable course knowledge system. The thesis is a method claim, not a learning-outcomes claim. Section XI's opening paragraph states the disclaimer explicitly; the section's middle paragraphs describe the light-data plan and its instruments; the section's closing paragraph hands off the risk side to Section XII.

The disclaimer is load-bearing for the whole article's risk posture: Section XII's risk argument and Section XI's evaluation framing both depend on the article having said clearly that it is not asserting a treatment effect on student performance. Without the disclaimer, every reader of Sections III through VIII can construe the case study as an implicit performance claim, and every Bond et al. methodological objection becomes a fatal objection rather than an anticipated one.

The parenthetical in the workplan §1 bullet anticipates the Section XI design decision: the article either runs the study and reports learning outcomes, or it does not run the study and reports only the disclaimer plus the light-data plan. Workplan §5 (captured in `evidence-11-workplan-recommendation-method-plus-light-data.md`) endorses the second path.
