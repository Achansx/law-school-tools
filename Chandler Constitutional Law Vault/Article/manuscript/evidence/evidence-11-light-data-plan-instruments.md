Workplan §5 (lines 155 through 161) gives the practical version of the light-data plan as four instruments. First, a brief anonymous Qualtrics or Google Forms survey at the end of the semester (or, depending on the Con Law I calendar, an already-running survey) with five Likert items on usefulness, navigation, trust, and overreliance, plus two open-ended items. Second, Netlify analytics for page-view counts by page type (Case versus Topic versus Lecture), search terms used, and time on page, anonymized. Third, three to five 30-minute think-aloud sessions with volunteer students preparing for the exam, in which students are asked to find an answer to a doctrinal question using the site and the session records what they click and what they say. Fourth, the entire data slice is framed in Section XI as usability and use-pattern data, never as learning-outcomes data; the framing is the methods-disciplinary commitment the section makes to a JLE audience.

The instruments share a common feature: each measures behavior or self-report on a reviewed static website, not learning outcomes. A Likert item on perceived overreliance is a perceived-overreliance measure; a navigation trace is a navigation measure; a think-aloud session is a use-strategy measure. None of them is a pre/post knowledge measure, and Section XI's prose must not let the four instruments together drift into an implicit learning-outcomes claim.
