Legal education already supports open educational resources, and the institutional implication of a course knowledge system is that it is complementary OER infrastructure rather than a competing publication channel. Two established projects publish openly licensed legal-education texts. H2O Open Casebooks is a free platform for making, sharing, and remixing open-licensed casebooks, developed and maintained by the Library Innovation Lab at the Harvard Law School Library; its materials carry a Creative Commons BY-NC-SA license, it draws on the public-domain case corpus available through CourtListener and the Caselaw Access Project, and over one hundred faculty authors have published casebooks on it. CALI's eLangdell Press distributes peer-reviewed casebooks and course materials that it describes as "100% Free. Creative Commons licensed. Peer-reviewed." H2O and eLangdell publish the texts; the course knowledge system publishes the structure around the texts: the typed schema, the doctrinal map, the verified-source links, and the navigable course graph. A school that adopts the method adds a structural layer the existing OER projects do not provide rather than displacing them.

Per L-024 the transfer-of-inference clause names what generalizes and what does not. What carries across is the open-resource commitment to reusable, openly licensed teaching materials. What does not carry across is the object of publication: H2O and eLangdell publish finished casebook texts under license, while a course knowledge system publishes structure and links over a professor's own slides, public opinions, and original hypotheticals, not third-party casebook text.
