Workplan §5 line 162 captures the IRB framing decision as a fork the section cannot resolve unilaterally. If the professor and the law school's IRB administrator agree that the light-data work is human subjects research, the work runs through IRB as exempt or expedited and Section XI labels it as human-subjects research with the IRB approval number in a footnote. If they disagree, the work is conducted as student-experience feedback rather than as research, and Section XI labels it as such in the article. The two labels carry different methodological obligations: research has consent, data-handling, and reporting obligations the IRB protocol specifies; student-experience feedback has the obligations the law school's own feedback policies specify and cannot be reported in the article as research results.

The card preserves the decision as an open question the section's prose must hedge until the professor and the IRB administrator have agreed. Section XI's drafting cannot use either label confidently without that confirmation. The hedge is also an honest signal to JLE reviewers: the methodological care the article advocates extends to the article's own evaluation work.
