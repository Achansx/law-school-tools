## 1. Choi & Schwarcz, *AI Assistance in Legal Analysis*, 73 J. Legal Educ. 384 (2025)
**Footnotes:** [^8] (intro, I.D), [^154] (XI.D). Also the *see also* in [^8]: Choi, Hickman, Monahan & Schwarcz, *ChatGPT Goes to Law School*, 71 J. Legal Educ. 387 (2022).
**Article's proposition:** [^8]/I.D — "AI assistance can help weaker students more than stronger ones on law-school examinations, with a potential equalizing effect"; models proportional rigor without a controlled trial. [^154]/XI.D — a recent demonstration in this Journal's own pages that meaningful empirical work on AI and law-school learning "does not require a full randomized controlled trial."
**VERDICT: SUPPORTED.**
**Evidence:** SSRN/Minnesota landing + search confirm: bottom-of-class students saw large gains with GPT-4 while top students declined — "AI may have an equalizing effect." Methodology is a treatment/control exam experiment, not a full RCT. Published 73 J. Legal Educ. 384 (2025); starting page 384 confirmed in search and on the JLE landing. The 2022 *ChatGPT Goes to Law School* (71 J. Legal Educ. 387) confirmed verbatim: title + four-author byline (Choi, Hickman, Monahan, Schwarcz) on the jle.aals.org vol71/iss3/2 landing.
Sources: papers.ssrn.com/abstract_id=4539836; jle.aals.org/home/vol73/iss2/5/; jle.aals.org/home/vol71/iss3/2/.

## 3. Mertz, *The Language of Law School: Learning to "Think Like a Lawyer"* (OUP 2007)
**Footnotes:** [^13] (II.C), [^138] (X.B).
**Article's proposition:** [^13]/II.C — much of what a professor teaches happens in real-time Socratic exchange (framing moves, hedges, moment-by-moment shaping of how a case is read), and little of that performance lands in any returnable artifact. [^138]/X.B — "tape-recorded ethnography of first-year Contracts classrooms across eight U.S. law schools" documenting the case-method form as the through-line of 1L doctrinal instruction, supporting that the standard Facts/Holding/Analysis brief shape is the case-method's product, not a con-law artifact.
**VERDICT: SUPPORTED** (with a light scope note on [^138]).
**Evidence:** OUP page + Google Books: linguistic ethnography built on tape recordings/observations of 1L Contracts in eight law schools; documents how the Socratic method teaches students to "think like a lawyer" by shifting from moral/social to legal-authority framing — i.e., performed classroom interaction is the object of study.
**Scope note ([^138]):** Mertz supports the case-method-as-1L-through-line generalization; the specific "standard Facts/Holding/Analysis brief shape is the case-method's product" is the article's own synthesis layered on Mertz's case-method finding. Defensible, but the brief-shape specificity is a gloss, not a verbatim Mertz finding. Optional narrowing: cite Mertz for the case-method form being the 1L through-line, and attribute the brief-shape inference to the article.

## 15. Magesh et al., *Hallucination-Free? Assessing the Reliability of Leading AI Legal Research Tools*, 22 J. Empirical Legal Stud. 216 (2025)

**VERDICT: SUPPORTED.** Hallucination-rate characterization confirmed; **exact figure reported by source = 17%–33%** (matches the article verbatim).
**Evidence:** Stanford-hosted open PDF, abstract: the LexisNexis and Thomson Reuters tools "each hallucinate between 17% and 33% of [the time]"; "Over 1 in 6 of our queries caused Lexis+ AI and Ask Practical Law AI" to respond falsely, and "Westlaw... one-third." All three are retrieval-augmented/corpus-grounded legal tools. Vol. 22, p. 216 confirmed. Preprint arXiv:2405.20362.
Sources: dho.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/Legal_RAG_Hallucinations.pdf; onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jels.12413; arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362.
